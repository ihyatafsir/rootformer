ROOT احد   stratum=Ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root أ-ح-د denotes absolute, indivisible oneness — the negation of multiplicity, composition, and partition. Its foundational physical origin is the notion of a single, isolated unit that admits no second, no plurality, and no internal division.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal meaning is 'one' in the strict sense of absolute singularity — that which is neither paired nor composed, neither divisible nor repeatable. It is the pure unit that excludes all others.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In philosophical and theological usage, الأحد is the divine attribute of absolute unity (al-Aḥadiyyah), signifying that God is one without composition, without parts, without multiplicity, and without any second. It is distinguished from الواحد: the latter denotes oneness in contrast to duality, while الأحد denotes oneness that excludes all relation and plurality absolutely. In logic and metaphysics, it is applied to the indivisible point, the unique individual, and the first principle.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الأحد: الواحد الذي لا ثاني له، ولا يقبل القسمة ولا التركيب، ولا يوصف به غيره تعالى في الأحدية المطلقة.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-Aḥad: the One who has no second, who admits neither division nor composition, and none other than God is described by it in absolute oneness.

ROOT اذا   stratum=Ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: A deictic-temporal particle whose foundational semantic function is to point to an indeterminate future time; it is not a lexical root with a concrete physical origin but a grammatical operator (adāt) denoting futurity, and it may acquire conditional force when construed with a following verb.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal function of إذا is to designate any future time (al-zamān al-mustaqbal), whether indefinite or specified by context; it is the temporal particle of futurity, as opposed to إذ, which designates past time.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In scholastic and grammatical usage, إذا may be invested with the meaning of condition (sharṭ) and thereby govern the jussive (jazm) in poetry predominantly; it does not function as a conditional particle unless joined with ما, as in إذ ما. It is also used in logical and theological discourse to mark temporal contingency and future-oriented hypotheticals, without denoting a concrete entity or accident.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: إذا يعبر به عن كل زمان مستقبل، وقد يضمن معنى الشرط فيجزم به، وذلك في الشعر أكثر؛ و«إذ» يعبر به عن الزمان الماضي، ولا يجازى به إلا إذا ضم إليه «ما».
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Ifā denotes any future time, and it may be invested with conditional meaning, in which case it governs the jussive—this being more frequent in poetry; whereas idh denotes past time, and it is not used conditionally unless joined with mā.

ROOT اذن   stratum=MA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root is the bodily organ of hearing — the ear — conceived as the locus of auditory apprehension. From this concrete anatomical nucleus, the root radiates outward to denote the act of listening, the granting of permission (since one who is heard is thereby admitted), and the act of proclamation or warning (since a summons is issued to be heard).
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal, physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is the ear as a bodily member (al-jāriḥah), together with its homologue in inanimate objects — the loop or handle of a vessel, the ear of a pot, and the like — by virtue of structural resemblance. It also denotes the concrete act of hearing and listening (al-istimāʿ), and the verbal noun al-adhān and al-adhān signifying that which is heard.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and scholastically, the root is transferred (mustaʿār) to designate a person who listens excessively and credulously, so that he is called 'an ear' — as in the Qurʾānic 'They say: He is an ear' (Q 9:61), meaning his listening conduces to your good. It is further employed for the faculty of apprehension and intellection (al-ʿilm) insofar as hearing is the primary conduit of knowledge among us, as in 'Grant me leave' (Q 9:49) and 'And when your Lord proclaimed' (Q 14:7). The verb adhina denotes to listen, to grant permission, and to proclaim or warn; taʾadhdhana signifies to give prior notice of evil or to announce a matter — as when the sultan's herald proclaims something, he has thereby 'taʾadhdhana' it. The Qurʾānic 'in their ears is a heaviness' (Q 6:25) is an allusion to their ignorance, not to any defect in their hearing.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الأذن: الجارحة، وشبه به من حيث الحلقة أذن القدر وغيرها، ويستعار لمن كثر استماعه وقوله لما يسمع. وأذن: استمع. والأذن والأذان لما يسمع، ويعبر بذلك عن العلم، إذ هو مبدأ كثير من العلم فينا. وأذنته بكذا وآذنته بمعنى.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-adhun: the bodily organ [of hearing]; the loop of a pot and the like are likened to it by virtue of structural resemblance; and it is metaphorically applied to one who listens excessively and accepts whatever he hears. Adhina means 'he listened.' Al-adhan and al-adhān denote that which is heard, and by this is expressed the notion of knowledge, since hearing is the origin of much of our knowledge. Adhantuhu bi-kadhā and ādhantuhu are synonymous [i.e., 'I informed him of such-and-such'].

ROOT ارش   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root ارش is the infliction of a wound or injury (jarḥ) upon the body, from which arises a compensatory claim or indemnity for that injury.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is the act of wounding or injuring (jarḥ) and the taking of compensation (arsh) for that wound.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In scholastic and legal usage, the term is extended metaphorically to denote the indemnity or financial compensation (arsh) paid for a wound or injury, and by further extension, any monetary compensation for damages. It is also used in the context of reconciliation or settlement (ṣulḥ) where a payment is made to resolve a dispute.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الأَرْشُ: دِيَةُ الجِرَاحَاتِ، وَقَدْ أَرَشَ يَأْرِشُ أَرْشًا إِذَا جَرَحَ وَأَخَذَ الأَرْشَ.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-Arsh: the indemnity for wounds; one says arasha ya'rishu arshan when he wounded and took the indemnity.

ROOT ازر   stratum=MA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin of the root is the waist-wrapper or loincloth (al-izār) that a person binds around the waist as a covering garment; from this concrete act of girding and enclosing derives the sense of surrounding, supporting, and strengthening.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning is the izār: the garment wrapped around the waist, also called izārah and miʾzar; it denotes the act of wearing such a wrapper and the manner of girding it (al-izrah), as in the phrase 'he is excellent in his manner of wearing the izār' and 'for every Arab tribe there is an izrah by which they gird themselves.'
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: By metaphor, the izār is used as a euphemism for the wife, since she is as closely attached and covering as the waist-wrapper. From the physical act of girding, the root extends to the sense of strengthening and supporting: 'he girded his back with it' means he fortified him; 'he supported me against it' means he backed and aided me; and 'one who supports and cooperates' is derived from the same image of mutual backing. The theological and ethical usage centers on the idea of God strengthening and supporting the believer, and of humans supporting one another in truth and justice.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: أصل الأزر: الإزار الذي هو اللباس، يقال: إزار وإزارة ومئزر، ويكنى بالإزار عن المرأة. وشد به أزره، ومعه من يؤامره ويؤازره. وأردت كذا فآزرني عليه فلان إذا ظاهرك وعاونك. وإنه لحسن الإزرة، ولكل قوم من العرب إزرة يأتزرونها.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: The root of al-azr is the izār, which is a garment; it is said izār, izārah, and miʾzar, and the izār is used as a euphemism for the wife. One says 'he girded his back with it,' and 'with him is one who consults him and supports him.' 'I wanted such-and-such, and so-and-so supported me against it' means he backed and aided me. 'He is excellent in his izrah' refers to the manner of wearing the waist-wrapper, and every Arab tribe has an izrah by which they gird themselves.

ROOT بعثر   stratum=Ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is the violent inversion and scattering of earth or a container so as to expose and cast out what lies concealed within it — specifically, the turning over of grave soil and the ejection of its buried contents.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: To overturn the earth of graves, scatter their soil, and bring forth what is hidden inside them, whether treasure or the dead.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In theological and eschatological usage, the term denotes the resurrection event in which graves are breached and their occupants emerge — a metonymy for the final disclosure and dispersion of all that was concealed. Al-Rāghib notes that some grammarians erroneously analyzed the quadriliteral بعثر as a compound of بعث (to raise) and أثير (to stir up), but this derivational fallacy is rejected because it violates the established morphological integrity of the root.

ROOT بلز   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) centers on compact, solid bodily mass — a dense, thickset corporeal bulk, particularly of a woman, characterized by firm, well-knit flesh rather than mere largeness.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning, per Lisān al-ʿArab, is a woman who is بِلِز (biliz) or بِلِزّ (bilizz): bulky, thickset, and compactly fleshed (ḍakhma muktaniza). Al-Jawharī restricts بِلِز to the pattern فِعِل (fiʿil) with kasrah on both the first and second radicals, meaning simply 'bulky.' Thaʿlab noted that only two adjectival forms in Arabic occur on the pattern فِعِل: a woman بِلِز and a she-ass إِبِد. The root also yields جَمَل بَلَنْزى (jamal balanzā): a thick, coarse, heavy-bodied camel.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: No metaphorical, philosophical, or theological extension is attested in the classical lexica. The root remains confined to the concrete physical domain of dense corporeal bulk and coarse animal mass; it was never abstracted into scholastic or theological usage. Its sole figurative potential lies in the transferred application of بَلَنْزى to a camel, extending the notion of compact solidity from the human female body to the heavy, thick-set frame of the male camel.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: امرأَة بِلِزٌ وبِلِزٌّ: ضخمة مكتنزة. قال ثعلب: لم يأْت من الصفات على فِعِلٍ إِلاَّ حرفان: امرأَة بِلِزٌ وأَتان إِبِدٌ. وجَمَل بَلَنْزى: غليظ.
