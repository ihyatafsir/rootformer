ROOT عنب   stratum=Ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical referent is the grape: the fruit of the vine (al-karm), and by extension the vine itself. The root's primary ontological anchor is the concrete, edible berry produced by the grapevine, from which the secondary sense of a small pustule or blister (ʿunbah) is derived by structural resemblance.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal, physical meaning is the grape: the well-known fruit of the vine, both individually as a single berry (ʿinabah) and collectively as grapes (ʿinab). It also denotes the grapevine itself (al-karm). The plural forms are aʿnāb and ʿinab. The diminutive/singulative ʿunbah denotes a single grape-berry, and by literal extension a pustule or blister on the body, due to its resemblance in shape to a grape.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In the Qurʾānic and theological register, ʿinab appears strictly in its literal botanical sense within enumerations of divine bounty: 'And from the fruits of the date-palm and grapes' (al-Naḥl 16:67); 'a garden of date-palms and grapes' (al-Isrāʾ 17:91); 'gardens of grapes' (al-Raʿd 13:4); 'orchards and grapes' (al-Nabaʾ 78:32); 'and grapes and clover and olives' (ʿAbasa 80:28); 'two gardens of grapes' (al-Kahf 18:32). No metaphorical or scholastic technical usage is attested in the classical lexical sources. The only extended application is the concrete anatomical analogy: ʿunbah as a blister or pustule, which remains a literal resemblance-based usage rather than a philosophical metaphor.

ROOT غرا   stratum=Ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is glue or adhesive substance (al-ghirāʾ), derived from fish, used to bind and attach one thing to another; hence the primary sense of sticking, adhering, and cleaving.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning is the act of gluing or pasting, as in غَرَوْتُ الجِلْدَ (I glued the hide), and the substance itself, الغِراء (glue), which causes one thing to adhere to another.

In genealogical and social discourse, the plural أَفْخَاذ designates the major sub-lineages or branches of a tribe, by analogy of the tribal body to a physical body whose limbs are its constituent clans. In juridical and lexical classification, the term may denote a portion or division of a larger whole, reflecting the part-whole relation inherent in the limb. The passive participle مَفْخُوذٌ is applied to one whose thigh has been injured, and by extension to any structure or entity metaphorically 'wounded' in its supporting member.

ROOT فرم   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root فرم is the act of constricting, narrowing, or tightening an orifice—specifically the vagina—by means of a medicinal agent or physical obstruction, thereby inducing a state of constriction (taḍayyuq).
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: According to Asās al-Balāghah, the literal physical meaning is the use of a drug or medicinal substance (farām) to constrict the vagina, as in the phrase 'istaframat al-marʾah' (the woman used a constricting agent). The root also appears in the proverbial expression 'more abject than the faram of a slave-girl,' referring to the medicinal plug used for vaginal constriction.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In metaphorical and scholastic usage, the root extends to any act of tightening, narrowing, or restricting, whether physical or abstract. The phrase 'ibn al-mustafrimah bi-ʿajam al-zabīb' (son of the woman who constricted herself with grape seeds) in the ḥadīth of ʿAbd al-Malik employs the root as a derogatory allusion to base origin, metaphorically linking physical constriction to moral or social degradation. The variant form 'tafrīm' (with mīm) is used interchangeably with 'tafrīb' (with bāʾ) in the sense of narrowing the woman's orifice.

-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-farm and al-firām: a medicinal substance by which a woman constricts [her vagina]. A woman who is farmāʾ or mustafrimah is one who places a drug in her vulva in order to narrow it.

ROOT فطن   stratum=mA

-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: To be acutely aware of, to grasp a matter with mental sharpness, to perceive something with alert intelligence; said of a person who is keenly attentive and quick to understand (فَطِنٌ), and of the act of making someone else attentive or sharpening their understanding (تفطين).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In scholastic and theological usage, فِطْنَة denotes the faculty of intellectual apprehension (idrāk) by which the rational soul grasps intelligible meanings, distinguished from mere sensory perception. The verb فَطَّنَ (to make someone attentive) is used in pedagogical and ethical contexts to signify the cultivation of the rational faculty through instruction and discipline.
