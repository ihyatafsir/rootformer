ROOT ختت   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is the act of piercing or thrusting with spears in rapid, successive, overlapping strikes — a concentrated, repeated penetration that overwhelms the target.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is: thrusting or piercing with spears in a rapid, successive manner (al-ṭaʿn bi-l-rimāḥ mudārakan).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorical and derived usages include: (1) a feeling of languor, weakness, or slackness that a person experiences in the body (al-khataṭ: futūr yajiduhu al-insān fī badanihi); (2) a man's becoming shy and silent (akhatta al-rajul: istaḥyā wa-sakata), denoting a retreat into reticence and bashfulness.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الخَتُّ: الطَّعْنُ بالرماح مُدارَكاً. والخَتَتُ: فُتُور يَجِدُه الإِنسان في بدنه. وأَخَتَّ الرجلُ: اسْتَحْيا وسَكَتَ.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-khatt: thrusting with spears in rapid succession. Al-khatat: a languor or weakness that a person feels in his body. Akhatta al-rajul: he became shy and silent.

ROOT خرعب   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin centers on the notion of a fresh, supple, and elevated offshoot—specifically a tender, sap-rich branch or shoot that rises prominently from a tree, and by extension, a freshly cut slice or segment of a moist, fleshy vegetable or fat.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to the classical lexicographers is: a piece or slice of a gourd, cucumber, or fat; and a tender, succulent, elevated branch or twig of a tree, especially one of the current year's growth.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: While the root is not attested in Al-Mufradāt or Asās al-Balāghah, its metaphorical and scholastic extensions are implicit in the classical material: the term may be applied to a young, slender, and graceful person (likened to a tender branch), or to any fresh, moist, and easily broken substance. In theological or philosophical discourse, it could serve as a concrete image for accidental qualities that are ephemeral and rooted in material substance, though no formal scholastic usage is recorded.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الخُرْعُوبةُ: القِطْعةُ من القَرْعةِ والقِثَّاءِ والشَّحْمِ. والخَرْعَبُ والخُرْعُوبُ والخُرْعُوبةُ: الغُصْنُ لسَنَتِه، وقيل: هو القَضِيبُ السامِقُ الغَضُّ، وقيل: هو القَضِيبُ الناعِمُ الحديثُ.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-Khurʿūbah: a piece of gourd, cucumber, or fat. Al-Kharʿab, al-Khurʿūb, and al-Khurʿūbah: a branch of the current year's growth; it is also said to be a tall, fresh twig; and it is said to be a tender, new branch.

ROOT خشم   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin of the root خ ش م centers on the nasal cavity and its internal structures—specifically the bony and cartilaginous passages, vessels, and membranes situated between the nostrils and the brain. This anatomical locus is the seat of olfactory perception and the point at which the sense of smell is either enabled or obstructed. From this concrete physical referent, the root's primary semantic charge is the obstruction, alteration, or loss of olfactory function, and by extension, the alteration of odors themselves.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is the nasal passage and its internal anatomy (al-khayāshīm), and the condition of a man who is akhsham—one who cannot detect odors due to a blockage or defect in his nasal passages. The wind or odor is said to penetrate (tasawwara) into the khayāshīm, i.e., the olfactory recesses of the nose.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorical and extended usages include: (1) the alteration or putrefaction of meat (khashima al-laḥm / akhshama), where the change in odor is conceptualized as a kind of nasal obstruction or corruption affecting the meat itself; (2) the theological and philosophical notion of olfactory privation (khasham) as a defect in the faculty of smell, analogous to blindness in the eye or deafness in the ear—a privation of a natural power, not merely a negation; (3) in scholastic physiology, the khayāshīm are described as the fine vessels or membranes at the extremity of the nose, connecting to the brain, serving as the instrument of the olfactory spirit (rūḥ al-shamm); (4) by extension, any blockage or obstruction in a channel of perception or communication may be termed khasham, though this remains a secondary, analogical usage.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: خَشِمَ اللحمُ خَشَماً وأَخْشَمَ: تغيرت رائحته. والخَيْشُوم من الأَنف: ما فوق نُخْرَتِهِ من القَصَبة وما تحتها من خَشارِمِ رأْسه، وقيل: الخَياشِيمُ غَراضيف في أَقصى الأَنف بينه وبين الدماغ، وقيل: هي عُروق.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Khashima al-laḥm khashaman wa-akhshama: its odor became altered. Al-khayshūm of the nose: that which is above the nostril from the bridge and below it from the cartilaginous parts of its head. It is said: al-khayāshīm are the fine membranes at the extremity of the nose between it and the brain; and it is said: they are veins.

ROOT خضب   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root خ-ض-ب is the act of dyeing or staining a surface—specifically human hair or skin—with a coloring substance such as henna (ḥinnāʾ) or katam, thereby altering its visible hue toward red, yellow, or another color. The root's primary semantic nucleus is the material application of pigment to a corporeal substrate, producing a perceptible chromatic change.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: According to Asās al-Balāghah, the literal (ḥaqīqah) meaning is the direct, physical application of dye (khiḍāb) to hair or hand, as in 'khaḍaba shaʿrahu wa yadahu bi-l-khiḍāb' (he dyed his hair and hand with dye), yielding 'kaffin khaḍīb' (a dyed palm) and 'banānun mukhaḍḍab' (dyed fingertips). It also denotes the vessel used for dyeing, 'al-mikhḍab' (the dyeing tub or basin), and the cloths employed in the process, 'makhāḍib' (dye rags). The verb forms include 'khaḍaba' (he dyed), 'ikhtaḍaba' (he dyed himself), and 'takhaḍḍaba' (he applied dye repeatedly or intensively).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In metaphorical and scholastic usage, the root extends beyond literal dyeing to signify any alteration of color or appearance, even by non-dye substances, as when Lisān al-ʿArab notes 'ghayyara lawnahu bi-ḥumratin aw ṣufratin aw ghayrihimā' (he changed its color to red, yellow, or otherwise). The active participle 'khaḍīb' is applied metaphorically to a star or celestial body, as in 'ṭalaʿat al-kaff al-khaḍīb wa hiya najm' (the dyed palm rose, which is a star), where the reddish hue of the star is likened to a henna-dyed hand. The intensive nominal form 'khaḍiba' describes a woman who dyes herself frequently, indicating habitual action. The root thus acquires a secondary semantic layer of transformative coloration, whether literal or analogical, without losing its concrete material basis.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الخِضابُ: ما يُخْضَبُ به مِن حِنَّاءٍ وكَتَمٍ ونحوه؛ وخَضَبَ الشيءَ يَخْضِبُهُ خَضْباً وخَضَّبَهُ: غيَّرَ لَوْنَه بحُمْرَةٍ أو صُفْرَةٍ أو غيرِهما.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-khiḍāb is that with which one dyes, such as henna, katam, and the like; khaḍaba al-shayʾa yakḥḍibuhu khaḍban and khaḍḍabahu mean he changed its color to red, yellow, or another hue.

In theological contexts, it may symbolize a deficiency in apprehension (idrāk) of divine realities, though this is not a primary Quranic usage.

ROOT خلا   stratum=Ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is spatial and temporal emptiness: a place or thing devoid of any occupant, structure, or content, hence 'to be empty, vacant, unoccupied.'
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning is the state of a place or thing being empty and devoid of any being or object, as in 'خلا المكان' (the place became empty) and 'الخلاء' (the open, unsheltered void).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorical and scholastic applications extend to temporal passage (خلا الزمان: time elapsed and departed), to the absence of precedent or peer (خلت من قبله الرسل: messengers have passed away before him), and to moral or communal vacancy (خلت من قبلهم المثلات: exemplary punishments occurred before them). The root also yields the theological notion of divine solitude (الخلوة) and the philosophical concept of privation (الخلاء) as the absence of substance in a locus.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: خَلا المكانُ والشيءُ يَخْلُو خُلُوّاً وخَلاءً وأَخْلَى إِذا لم يكن فيه أَحد ولا شيء فيه، وهو خالٍ. والخَلاءُ من الأَرض: قَرارٌ خالٍ. واسْتَخْلَى: كخَلا.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: A place or thing becomes empty (خلا) with the verbal noun خُلُوّ and خَلاء, and one may say أَخْلَى when there is no one and nothing in it; it is then خالٍ (empty). الخَلاء from the earth is a vacant, level tract. اِسْتَخْلَى is synonymous with خلا.

ROOT خوط   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root خوط is the tender, supple, freshly grown branch or shoot of a tree—specifically the pliant young twig that emerges in a single growing season, characterized by its softness, flexibility, and slender form.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: According to Asās al-Balāghah, the literal (ḥaqīqah) meaning of خوط is the soft, tender branch (al-ghuṣn al-nāʿim). It is used concretely to denote a pliant young shoot or twig, as in the phrase 'qad kāla-khūṭ' (it curved like a tender branch), and in the plural khīṭān, as in the poetic image of slender figures behind walls 'like tender branches' (qadūd ka-l-khīṭān).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In metaphorical (majāz) and scholastic usage, خوط is extended to denote any slender, graceful, and flexible form, particularly the elegant stature or figure of a person. The imagery of the tender branch serves as a simile for youthful beauty, supple movement, and graceful bearing. In theological and philosophical discourse, the term may be employed to illustrate the concept of innate disposition (fiṭrah) or natural growth, where the tender shoot symbolizes the initial, unspoiled state of created beings before external influences shape them. It also appears in literary and rhetorical contexts to evoke the idea of delicate, nascent potentiality.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الخُوطُ: الغُصْنُ الناعِمُ، وقيل: الغُصن لِسَنةٍ، وقيل: هو كلُّ قَضِيبٍ ما كان؛ والجمع خِيطانٌ.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-khūṭ: the tender branch; it is said to be a branch of one year's growth; and it is said to be any twig whatsoever; the plural is khīṭān.

ROOT داف   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin is the act of walking or moving with a swaying, oscillating, or side-to-side motion, often likened to the undulating gait of a camel or a ship tossing on waves.

ROOT دثر   stratum=MA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin of the root is the act of covering or being covered by an outer garment (dithār) that is worn over the inner garment (shīʿār), thereby concealing what lies beneath. It is also used for an abode that has become 'dāthir' (effaced), i.e., its traces have been obliterated by time, and for a person who is 'dathūr' of wealth, meaning he is an excellent manager of it, as if he covers and protects it. -- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: By metaphorical extension (majāz), the term is applied to any entity—human, animal, or figurative—marked by audacious initiative and unhesitating advance; the lion is the archetypal instantiation, and the semantic core of 'charging forward' is transferred from physical motion to the quality of dauntless boldness. No theological or philosophical technical usage is attested in the classical lexica.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الدَّلْهَثُ والدُّلَاهِثُ والدِّلْهَاثُ: كلُّه السريعُ الجريءُ المُقْدِمُ من الناس والإِبل. والدِّلْهَاثُ: الأَسَدُ. قال أَبو منصور: كأَنَّ أَصله من الاندلاث، وهو التَّقَدُّم، فزيدت الهاء.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-dalhath, al-dulāthith, and al-dilhāth all denote the swift, bold, forward-charging one among people and camels. Al-dilhāth also denotes the lion. Abū Manṣūr said: it is as though its origin is from al-indilāth, which is forward advance, with the hāʾ augmented.
