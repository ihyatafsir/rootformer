ROOT دمرغ   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root denotes an extreme, saturated intensity of a color quality — specifically a concentrated, almost overwhelming redness or whiteness — as if the hue has been fully infused and intensified in the object.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning is a man of intense, saturated redness; and, by extension, a stark, intensely white object or person (أبيض دُمَّرِغ), indicating the utmost degree of whiteness.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: No established metaphorical, philosophical, or theological usage is attested in the classical lexica; the term remains confined to the concrete sensory domain of color intensity, with no abstract or scholastic extension recorded.

ROOT دهن   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin of the root د-ه-ن is the act of moistening, coating, or anointing a surface with a fatty, oily substance, thereby producing a slight, superficial wetness or glaze. This concrete sense of applying oil to the head or body underlies all subsequent literal and figurative extensions.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal, physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is the application of oil or grease to the head or another surface (دهن رأسه، ودهنه، وادهن، وتدهن), as well as the oil itself (الدُّهْن) and the vessel that contains it (مدهن). It also denotes a tract of sandy ground (الدهناء) and a place where a small amount of water collects (مدهن).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and scholastically, the root extends to: (1) the residue or dregs of olive oil (دردي الزيت) in the Qur'anic image 'فكانت وردة كالدهان' (Q 55:37); (2) a she-camel with scant milk (الدهين), either because she yields only as much as is needed for anointing (active participle) or because she is metaphorically 'anointed' with milk due to its scarcity (passive participle); (3) rain that moistens the earth lightly (دهن المطر الأرض), akin to oiling the head; (4) a euphemistic or ironic expression for beating with a stick (دهنه بالعصا), comparable to 'مسحته بالسيف' and 'حييته بالرمح'; and (5) the Qur'anic expression 'تنبت بالدهن' (Q 23:20), where the olive tree is described as producing oil, a teleological nuance pointing to its beneficial essence.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الدُّهْن: معروف. دَهَن رأْسه وغيره يَدْهُنه دَهْناً: بلَّه، والاسم الدُّهْن، والجمع أَدْهان ودِهان. والمِدْهَن: ما يجعل فيه الدهن. والدَّهين: الناقة القليلة اللبن. ودهن المطر الأرض: بلها بللاً يسيراً.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Oil is well known. To anoint one's head or something else, he oils it with anointing: he moistened it. The noun is oil, and the plural is oils or greases. The oil container is that in which oil is placed. The dahīn is a she-camel with scant milk. Rain anointed the earth: it moistened it with a slight moisture.

ROOT ذرع   stratum=MA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin of the root is the forearm—the limb extending from the elbow to the tip of the middle finger—and, by extension, the act of measuring or spanning with that limb.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning is the forearm as a bodily member, and the act of measuring a thing (such as cloth) by means of the forearm; also, the wooden rod or cubit measure used for such measurement.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and scholastically, the root extends to: (1) the measured object itself (al-madrūʿ), i.e., that which is measured by the forearm; (2) a chain whose length is measured in cubits, as in the Qurʾānic phrase 'a chain of seventy cubits'; (3) a swift-paced horse or a wide-stepping animal, from the image of extending the forelegs; (4) a person who stretches out the arm in walking or in a transaction (to sell); (5) a woman quick-handed at spinning; (6) a palm tree of the height of a man; (7) camels wading into water with their forelegs; (8) a man seeking help with his hand in his effort; and (9) a bearer of good news gesturing with his hand.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الذِّراعُ: ما بين طرَف المِرْفق إِلى طرَفِ الإِصْبَع الوُسْطى، أُنثى وقد تذكَّر. ويعبر به عن المذروع، أي: الممسوح بالذراع.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: The forearm: that which lies between the tip of the elbow and the tip of the middle finger; it is feminine, though it may be treated as masculine. It is also used to denote the measured object, that is, that which is measured by the forearm.

ROOT ذفل   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root denotes a thin, fluid, resinous substance — specifically the light, unthickened stage of tar (qaṭirān) before it congeals into the dense pitch (khaḍkhāḍ). Its foundational physical image is that of a viscous liquid exudate, prior to coagulation.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal, physical referent is thin tar (al-qaṭirān al-raqīq) — the runny, uncondensed resinous fluid that precedes the thickened pitch known as khaḍkhāḍ.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: No attested metaphorical, philosophical, or theological extension is recorded in the classical lexica; the root remains confined to its concrete material sense. Any figurative application (e.g., to moral defilement or obscurity) would be an extra-lexical extension not sanctioned by the classical sources.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الذَّفْل والذِّفْل: القَطِران الرقيق الذي قبل الخَضْخاض.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-dhafl and al-dhifl: the thin tar that exists prior to the thickened pitch (al-khaḍkhāḍ).

ROOT ذمر   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root ذمر is the act of combining blame or rebuke with urgent incitement—specifically, a leader's sharp censure of his troops to spur them into battle, or a shepherd's tactile stimulation of a newborn camel's neck to rouse it. The root thus denotes a forceful, often harsh, arousal that fuses reproach with motivational propulsion.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: According to Asās al-Balāghah, the literal physical meaning is: to incite someone toward a matter by blaming him, so that he exerts himself therein. It is said: the commander yudhammiru (incites) his companions in war—he makes them hear what is disagreeable in order to sharpen them. And they are seen yatadhāmarūn (mutually inciting) in war. A shepherd dhamara the newborn camel: he touched its fahqah (the base of the neck where the head joins the neck) to determine its sex. Hence the term mudhammir is used for the instrument or person who performs this, and for camels it is like a midwife for humans.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and scholastically, the root extends to: (1) self-reproach combined with self-motivation—one who yatadhammar blames himself for negligence while simultaneously energizing himself to avoid future remissness; (2) tribal or personal honor defended with zeal, as in ḥāmī al-dhimār (protector of the sacred domain), where failure to defend would incur blame and rebuke; (3) courage and valor, as in dhammar min al-adhmār (a brave one among the brave); (4) in the ḥadīth of ʿAlī: 'The Devil has dhammara his party'—i.e., incited and emboldened them, blending censure with encouragement.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الذَّمْرُ: اللَّوْمُ والحَضُّ معاً. ذَمَرَهُ يَذْمُرُهُ ذَمْراً: لامَهُ وحَضَّهُ وحَثَّهُ. وتَذَمَّرَ هو: لامَ نفسَه. وهو ذَمَرٌ من الأَذْمَارِ: شجاع. وذَمَرَ الراعي السَّلِيلَ: مَسَّ فَهْقَتَهُ. والمُذَمِّرُ للإِبل كالقابلة للناس. وهو حامي الذِّمار إِذا حَمَى ما لو لم يَحْمِهِ لِيمَ وعُنِّفَ.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-dhamr is blame and incitement together. Dhamara-hu yadhmuru-hu dhamran: he blamed him and incited and urged him on. And tadhammara: he blamed himself. He is dhamar among the adhmār: brave. The shepherd dhamara the newborn camel: he touched its fahqah (base of the neck). The mudhammir for camels is like the midwife for humans. He is the protector of the dhimār when he defends what, if he did not defend it, he would be blamed and rebuked for.

ROOT ذوب   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical process of a solid substance losing its rigidity and flowing, i.e., the liquefaction of fat, snow, or any congealed body through the application of heat or an external agent.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal, sensory meaning: the melting or dissolving of a solid (such as fat, snow, or ice) into a liquid state, and the transitive act of causing such melting.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorical and philosophical extensions: the dissolution or wasting away of bodily strength, the softening of a hard heart, the fading of resolve, or the vanishing of wealth; also used in theological contexts to describe the annihilation of created things before the divine command, and in logic to denote the resolution of a composite into its simple elements.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الذَّوْبُ: ضِدُّ الجُمُودِ. ذَابَ يَذُوبُ ذَوْبًا وذَوَبَانًا: نَقِيضُ جَمَدَ. وأَذَابَهُ غَيْرُهُ، وأَذَبْتُهُ، وذَوَّبْتُهُ، واسْتَذَبْتُهُ: طَلَبْتُ مِنْهُ ذَاكَ.

ROOT رول   stratum=mA

-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: According to Asās al-Balāghah, the literal physical meaning is the flowing saliva or foam of a horse or beast, and the act of an animal lowering itself to urinate; also the anointing or moistening of the head with oil and of bread with clarified butter or condiment.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorical and extended usages include: the flowing of saliva into a pouch or container; the emergence of teeth, especially the 'rāwūl' (a tooth that is a companion or adjacent to another tooth); and the phrase 'رُوَالٌ رَائِل' (flowing saliva) modeled on 'شِعْرٌ شَاعِر' for intensification. ROOT زحلف   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin of the root زحلف is the act of sliding or slipping downward, specifically the smooth, frictionless descent over a surface, as exemplified by children sliding from the top of a hill to its bottom. This core image of downward slippage underlies all subsequent literal and figurative applications.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah (delineated from Lisān al-ʿArab) is the trace or track left by children sliding down a hill, and by extension, the act of sliding itself. It denotes a smooth, downward glide, often on a slippery surface, and is synonymous with زحلوقة (zuhlūqah) in the dialect of the ʿĀliyah tribe, while Tamīm pronounces it with a qāf (زحلوقة).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: While not attested in Asās al-Balāghah, the metaphorical and scholastic extensions of زحلف are derived from its literal sense of sliding. These include: (1) the slippage or deviation from a straight path, whether physical or moral; (2) the rapid, uncontrolled descent into error or sin; (3) in philosophical and theological contexts, the instability of the soul or intellect when it loses its footing on the path of virtue or truth, leading to a fall from grace or a lapse in judgment. The term may also be applied to the slippery nature of worldly affairs and the transience of material existence.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الزُّحْلُوفَةُ: كالزُّحْلُوقَة، وقد تَزَحْلَفَ. وهي آثارُ تَزَلُّجِ الصِّبيان من فوقِ التَّلِّ إلى أَسْفَله، وهي لغة أهل العالية، وتميمٌ تقوله بالقاف، والجمع زحالِفُ وزَحالِيفُ.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-Zuḥlūfah: like al-zuḥlūqah, and one says tazaḥlafa. It is the traces of children sliding from the top of a hill to its bottom; it is the dialect of the people of al-ʿĀliyah, while Tamīm pronounces it with a qāf; the plural is zaḥālif and zaḥālīf.

ROOT زخا   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root denotes a toponymic designation (a place-name) attested in the poetry of Hudhayl, transmitted as زُخَيّات (a diminutive nominal form); its ontological status is onomastic rather than predicative, lacking a productive physical or eventive Aṣl al-Wadʿ in the classical lexical corpus.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: A proper place-name (موضع) occurring in Hudhaylī verse in the diminutive form زُخَيّات; the reading الزَّوَاخِي transmitted by some authorities is judged by Ibn Sīdah to be a scribal corruption (تصحيف) of the correct زُخَيّات with zāy and khāʾ.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: No metaphorical, philosophical, or theological extension is attested; the root remains confined to onomastic reference, and any scholastic or figurative application would be an unwarranted extrapolation beyond the classical lexical evidence.
