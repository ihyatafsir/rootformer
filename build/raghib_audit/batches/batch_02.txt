ROOT جنز   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin is the act of covering, concealing, or shrouding an object — a concrete operation of overlaying something so as to hide it from view.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: To cover or conceal a thing; to shroud it. The verb جَنَزَ الشيءَ يَجْنِزُه جَنْزاً denotes the literal physical act of draping or wrapping an entity so that it is hidden.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In scholastic and theological usage, the root extends metaphorically to the shrouding of the dead — the ritual wrapping of a corpse in a burial shroud (تكفين) and the accompanying funeral prayer. The imperative form in the narration 'إِذا جَنَزْتُموها فآذِنُوني' ('when you have shrouded her, inform me') illustrates this transferred sense: the literal act of covering becomes the ritual preparation of the deceased for burial, and by further extension, the funeral procession itself. The root thus bridges the physical act of concealment and the theological domain of death, ritual purity, and eschatological transition.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: جَنَزَ الشيءَ يَجْنِزُه جَنْزاً: ستره. وذكروا أَن النَّوَار لما احْتُضِرَت أَوْصَت أَن يصلي عليها الحسن، فقيل له في ذلك، فقال: إِذا جَنَزْتُموها فآذِنُوني.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: He covered or concealed the thing, covering it with a janz. It is related that when al-Nawār was on her deathbed, she stipulated that al-Ḥasan should pray over her; when this was mentioned to him, he said: 'When you have shrouded her, inform me.'

-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is the narrowing or constriction of a space or the chest, as in 'ḥaraja ṣadruhu ḥarajan' (his chest became constricted), and the act of driving or forcing someone into a narrow place, as in 'aḥrajahu ilā kadhā' (he forced him into such a situation) and 'aḥraja al-sabʿ ilā maḍīq' (he drove the beast of prey into a narrow passage).

ROOT حرذن   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is a small, low-creeping creature — a lizard (ʿaẓāʾah) — and, by extension, a beast of burden ridden to the point of total exhaustion, i.e., a quadruped reduced to its last remnant of strength.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah (as delineated from Lisān al-ʿArab) is: a small lizard (ʿaẓāʾah), specifically the ḥirzaw n, a creeping reptile; and, in the domain of camels, a riding camel that is mounted continuously until no strength remains in it.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorical, philosophical, and theological usages: The term is not attested in Al-Mufradāt or Asās al-Balāghah as a technical philosophical or theological concept. Its majāz potential lies in the extended image of the ridden camel: a being or faculty worn down to its last remainder (baqiyyah), i.e., reduced to utter exhaustion or depletion. In scholastic Arabic, it could be applied figuratively to a person or faculty that has been overworked until no residual capacity (baqiyyah) remains, but this is an analogical extension, not a canonical technical usage.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الحِرْذَوْنُ: العَظَاءَةُ، مَثَّلَ به سيبويه وفسره السيرافي عن ثعلب. والحِرْذَوْنُ من الإبل: الذي يُرْكَبُ حتى لا تَبقى فيه بقيَّة. الجوهري: الحِرْذَوْنُ دُوَيْبَّة.

-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is the act of restraining, hindering, or preventing a being from acting or moving — a privative condition imposed upon a subject, conceived as a blockage or confinement of its capacity for free disposal and locomotion.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning is the prevention or hindrance of a person or animal from acting, moving, or disposing of affairs freely; it denotes a state of being held back, confined, or rendered incapable of motion or independent action.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In metaphorical and scholastic usage, the root extends to any impediment — whether physical, moral, or circumstantial — that obstructs the actualization of a faculty or the execution of an intention. It may denote the inhibition of the soul's appetitive or irascible powers, the blockage of a process, or the privation of a perfection due to an external or internal obstacle. Theologically, it can signify the withholding of divine assistance or the imposition of a trial that restricts the servant's capacity, though such usage remains analogical and must not be conflated with the literal root sense of physical restraint.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الحَظْل: المَنْع من التصرّف والحركةِ، حَظَل يَحْظِل ويَحْظُل حَظْلاً وحِظْلاناً وحَظَلاناً.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-Ḥaẓl: prevention from free disposal and movement; ḥaẓala (yaḥẓil, yaḥẓul) — ḥaẓlan, ḥiẓlān, and ḥaẓalān.

ROOT حفص   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) centers on the act of gathering, collecting, or bringing together a thing into a compact mass; a secondary, less authoritative strand denotes casting or throwing something from the hand, though the classical lexicographers explicitly judge the cognate root with ḍād (حفض) as the superior form for that latter sense.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning is the gathering and collecting of a thing (حَفَصَ الشيءَ: جَمَعَه), and the resulting substance so gathered is termed الحُفَاصة. A marginal literal usage denotes casting a thing away (حَفَصَ الشيءَ: أَلْقَاه), but Ibn Sīdah and Ibn Barrī both affirm that the variant with ḍād (حفض) is the higher and more correct form for the sense of throwing down.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: No established metaphorical, philosophical, or theological application is attested in the classical philological corpus. The root remains confined to its concrete physical signification of gathering/collecting, with the throwing sense treated as a dialectal or orthographic variant subordinate to the ḍād form. Consequently, no majāz extension into abstract, scholastic, or theological domains is warranted by the classical sources.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: حَفَصَ الشيءَ يَحْفِصُه حَفْصاً: جَمَعَه. والحُفَاصةُ: اسمُ ما حُفِصَ. وحَفَصَ الشيءَ: أَلْقَاه، قال ابن سيده: والضاد أَعْلى.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Ḥafaṣa al-shayʾa yaḥfiṣuhu ḥafṣan: he gathered it together. Al-ḥufāṣah: the noun denoting that which has been gathered. And ḥafaṣa al-shayʾa: he cast it away; Ibn Sīdah said: the ḍād variant is the higher and more correct form.

ROOT حكر   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin of the root حكر is the act of withholding, confining, or hoarding a commodity—specifically foodstuffs—by collecting and retaining it in expectation of scarcity and price inflation. It denotes a deliberate, self-serving retention that restricts circulation and access.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: According to Asās al-Balāghah, the literal meaning is the hoarding of food (احتكار الطعام), i.e., its retention and confinement for the purpose of selling at a higher price during times of scarcity. It also literally denotes a person who monopolizes something and acts exclusively with it (المحتجن للشيء المستبد به), and can describe difficulty, crookedness, or harshness in social interaction (عسر والتواء وسوء معاشرة).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and in scholastic usage, the root extends to any monopolistic or exclusive appropriation (استبداد) by which an individual controls a good or resource to the exclusion of others. It can also denote contentious disputation (مماراة) and hostile argumentation (مناكرة ومحاكرة). In economic and ethical theology, it signifies the sin of hoarding (احتكار) as a violation of communal welfare and trust.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الحَكْرُ: ادِّخارُ الطعام للتَّرَبُّصِ، وصاحبُه مُحْتَكِرٌ. والاحْتِكارُ: جمع الطعام ونحوه مما يؤكل واحتباسُه انتظارَ وقتِ الغَلاءِ به.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-ḥakr is the storing of food in anticipation [of scarcity]; one who does so is a muḥtakir. Al-iḥtikār is the gathering of food and similar edible goods and retaining them in expectation of a time of high prices.

ROOT حلبس   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is not attested in Al-Mufradāt; classical lexicography derives it from the semantic field of clinging, tenacious adherence and unyielding persistence, from which the meaning of courage and steadfastness is extrapolated.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is not attested; based on Lisān al-ʿArab, the ḥaqīqah is a person who clings firmly to something and does not let go, and by extension a brave, steadfast individual.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: The metaphorical and scholastic usage applies the root to denote moral courage and resoluteness, where the physical act of clinging is transferred to the soul's firm adherence to valor and endurance; the term may also be used for a persistent, inseparable companion or quality.

ROOT حنق   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is the constriction and compression of the belly against the spine, as seen in a lean, emaciated horse; from this concrete image of tight inward contraction, the root extends to the psychological constriction of intense inward rage.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is the adhesion of the belly to the back in a horse or other animal due to emaciation and leanness, as in the verse of Labīd: 'by a travel-worn steed, whose belly clung to its spine and hump.' It also denotes the compact, gathered head of grain before its awns and kernels emerge.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: The metaphorical and theological usage denotes intense anger and wrath (shiddat al-ightiyāẓ), conceived as a constriction of the self analogous to the physical contraction of the belly to the spine. The verb forms ḥaniqa (to be enraged), aḥnaqa (to enrage), and the intensive ḥanīq and muḥnaq (one filled with rage) are used for the psychological state. In scholastic contexts, it signifies the soul's violent inward compression caused by perceived injury, without implying any corporeal change in God; when attributed to God, it is understood as His just displeasure and punitive will, not as an emotion or accident.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الحَنَقُ: شِدَّةُ الاغْتِيَاظِ. وأَحْنَقَ الفرسُ وغيرهُ إذا التصقَ بطنهُ بصلبهِ ضُمْرًا.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Ḥanaq is intense wrath. Aḥnaqa al-faras or other animal means its belly adhered to its spine due to emaciation.

ROOT حوب   stratum=MA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is the sharp cry or call used to restrain and drive back camels (ḥawb as a deterrent cry), from which the root's semantic nucleus of restraint, prohibition, and consequent moral liability is derived.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning is the act of crying out to camels to restrain them (ḥāba ḥawban), and by extension, the state of being restrained or held back from something; also, the concrete kinship obligation or right (ḥawbah) that binds one to protect and provide for weak dependents, and the physical need or destitution (ḥawbah) that drives one to transgression.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and scholastically, it denotes sin, moral offense, and the burden of guilt (ithm); the act of scrupulously avoiding sin (taḥawwub); the sacred inviolable right or sanctity (ḥurmah) of a person that, if neglected, incurs sin; and the soul (ḥawbāʾ) as the locus of moral accountability, specifically the soul that incites to evil (al-nafs al-ammārah bi-l-sūʾ).

ROOT خبا   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin centers on concealment, hiding, and the act of rendering something invisible or sheltered from view. It encompasses both the physical act of covering or storing something away and the structural notion of a shelter (خِباء) that veils its occupants.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is the act of hiding, concealing, or storing something away (خبأ), and the physical structure of a tent made of wool or hair (خِباء) that provides concealment. It also includes the literal sense of a young woman kept secluded (مخبأة) and the vessel for storing vinegar (خابية).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and scholastically, the root extends to the theological domain: 'God brings forth the hidden' (والله يخرج الخبء), referring to His bringing forth rain from the sky and vegetation from the earth as hidden treasures. It also encompasses the psychological act of concealing one's thoughts or intentions (اختبأت له خبيأ), and the strategic concealment of one's opinion (خابأتك أي حاجبتك). The term خِباء is used metaphorically for any sheltering structure, and خبأة describes a woman who conceals herself after being seen.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الخِباءُ من الأَبنية: واحد الأَخْبية، وهو ما كان من وَبَر أَو صوف ولا يكون من شَعَر، وهو على عمودين أَو ثلاثة، وما فوقَ ذلك فهو بَيْت. والخبء: ما خفي، ومنه: والله يخرج الخبء.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-Khibāʾ is a type of structure: the singular of al-akhbiya (tents), and it is made of camel hair or wool, not of hair, and it is supported by two or three poles; anything larger is called a bayt (house). ROOT خبب   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin of the root خبب is rapid, oscillating locomotion—specifically, a gait in which a mount moves its right limbs together and then its left limbs together, or alternately swings forelegs and hindlegs, producing a swift, rocking, uneven motion. From this kinetic core radiate secondary physical senses: a long strip or fold of cloth (خُبَّة/خَبِيبة), and a cutaneous disease or mange (خَبَب/جَرَب) that spreads irregularly over the body.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: In its strict literal sense, خَبَب denotes a specific mode of running: the horse's simultaneous movement of all right limbs then all left limbs, or the alternating swing of forelegs and hindlegs; it is also used for the camel. Hence خَبَّت الدَّابَّةُ تَخُبُّ خَبّاً وخَبَباً وخَبِيباً: the mount moved with this gait. Literally, خُبَّة and خَبِيبة denote a long, extended strip or fold of cloth, and a garment described as خَبائب is one with such strips. Literally, خَبّ as a noun denotes mange or scabies (الجَرَب), and a man or woman afflicted is described as خَبٌّ or خَبَّة.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and scholastically, خبب extends from physical gait to moral and social domains. In the ḥadīth of ʿUmar, 'no one spoke Persian except he خَبَّ'—i.e., became corrupt or afflicted with a moral disease—and 'he did not خَبَّ except that his manliness departed.' Thus خَبَّ عليه عبده وأمته وامرأته means he corrupted or ruined them (أفسد). The verbal form أَخَبَّ فَرَسَهُ means he urged his horse into a swift gait; مَرُّوا مُخِبِّينَ describes travelers moving rapidly with their mounts. The root also yields خَبْخَبَة (a rustling or confused sound) and تَخَبْخَبَ (to become agitated or mixed), extending the oscillatory core to acoustic and psychological perturbation.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الخَبَبُ: ضَرْبٌ من العَدْوِ، وقيل: هو مِثْلُ الرَّمَلِ، وقيل: هو أَن يَنْقُل الفَرَسُ أَيامِنَه جميعاً وأَياسِرَه جميعاً، وقيل: هو أَن يُراوِحَ بين يديهِ ورجليهِ، وكذلك البعير؛ وقيل: الخَبَب السُّرْعَة. والخُبَّةُ والخَبِيبةُ: شِبْهُ طِيَّةٍ من الثوب مستطيلة. والخَبُّ: الجَرَبُ.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-Khabab is a mode of running; it is said to resemble the ramal; it is said to be the horse's simultaneous movement of all its right limbs and then all its left limbs; it is said to be the alternating swing between its forelegs and hindlegs, and likewise for the camel; it is also said that al-khabab is speed. Al-khubba and al-khabība denote a long, extended fold or strip of cloth. Al-khabb denotes mange.
