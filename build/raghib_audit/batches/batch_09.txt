ROOT فوخ   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root ف-و-خ is the emission and diffusion of a scent or odor, specifically the rising and spreading of a fragrant or malodorous exhalation through the air, akin to the root ف-و-ح. It denotes the effusion of a volatile substance that fills the surrounding atmosphere and may be perceived by the sense of smell, sometimes with a forceful or overpowering quality.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah (as inferred from Lisān al-ʿArab) is: the diffusion and rising of a smell or odor, whether pleasant (like musk) or foul, such that it spreads through the air; also, the emission of wind (flatulence) from a person, and the act of a smell taking away one's breath due to its intensity.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: While not attested in Asās al-Balāghah, the metaphorical and scholastic extensions may include: the diffusion of reputation or news (as a scent spreads), the emanation of spiritual or moral influence, and in theological contexts, the manifestation of divine attributes or the diffusion of sanctity. However, these are not classical attestations and remain speculative; the primary usage is literal sensory.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: فَاخَ المِسْكُ يَفُوخُ وَيَفِيخُ فَوْخًا وَفَوَخَانًا: سَطَعَ مِثْلَ فَاحَ. وَفَاخَ الرَّجُلُ يَفُوخُ فَوْخًا وَأَفَاخَ يُفِيخُ: خَرَجَتْ مِنْهُ رِيحٌ.

ROOT قبا   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root ق ب و is the act of gathering, collecting, or picking up a substance with the fingers or hand, specifically the harvesting of saffron and safflower. This concrete manual act of aggregation is the root's primary material sense, from which all subsequent applications derive by extension or restriction.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is the direct, manual gathering of a substance—such as saffron (زعفران) or safflower (عصفر)—by the fingers, i.e., picking or harvesting it. It also denotes the physical convergence or closing of the lips (القَبْوة: انضمام ما بين الشفتين).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: The metaphorical and scholastic extensions include: (1) The term القابية (al-qābiya) for a woman who gathers saffron, a nominal derivation from the literal act; (2) The garment known as القَباء (al-qabāʾ), a long outer robe or cloak, which is a lexical extension from the root's sense of 'gathering' or 'enclosing' the body, though morphologically distinct (madd). In philosophical and theological usage, the root does not appear in classical scholastic texts for substance or accident; its majāz remains confined to concrete and social domains.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: قَبا الشيءَ قَبْواً: جمعه بأَصابعه. والقَبْوةُ: انضمام ما بين الشفتين. والقَباء، ممدود، من الثياب.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: He gathered the thing with his fingers. Al-qabwa is the closing of the lips. Al-qabāʾ, with madd, is a type of garment.

ROOT قردحم   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root denotes a specific, rugged, and remote topographical location—a place-name (موضع) characterized by harsh, broken terrain. Its foundational physical origin is the concrete designation of a desolate, stony ground or a rugged tract of land, from which the abstract sense of scattering and dispersal derives by analogy: to be cast into such broken country is to be dispersed beyond recovery.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: A proper noun (اسم علم) for a specific rugged, stony place or tract of land. In its strict literal usage, it is a toponym, indeclinable (غير مصروف) as a place-name, denoting a harsh, broken terrain. The phrase ذهبوا شَعالِيل بِقِرْدَحْمةٍ means 'they scattered in every direction at Qirdahma,' i.e., they dispersed across a rugged, remote location.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: By metonymic extension (مجاز مرسل), the toponym is used to signify total, irrecoverable dispersal. The scholastic usage is idiomatic: 'they went off to Qirdahma' (ذهبوا بقردحمة) signifies that a group has scattered in all directions, never to regroup. The underlying trope is spatial: a rugged, remote place serves as the emblem of utter dispersion. Al-Liḥyānī records the variant قِنْدَحْرة and قِدَّحْرة in the same idiomatic sense, confirming that the root's semantic core is the notion of scattering into a harsh, broken landscape.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: قِرْدَحْمة: موضع. الفراء: ذهبوا شَعالِيل بِقِرْدَحْمةٍ أَي تفرقوا. قال ابن بري: وفي الغريب المصنف بِقِرْدَحْمةَ غير مصروف. وحكى اللحياني في نوادره: ذهب القوم بِقِنْدَحْرةٍ وقِنْدَحْرةٍ وقِدَّحْرة
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Qirdahma: a place. Al-Farrāʾ said: 'They went off in every direction at Qirdahma,' i.e., they scattered. Ibn Barrī said: In al-Gharīb al-Muṣannaf it appears as 'bi-Qirdahmata' in the indeclinable form. Al-Liḥyānī related in his Nawādir: 'The people went off to Qindahra, Qindahra, and Qiddahra' [in the same sense of dispersal].

-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is the act of hooding and sealing a bird of prey — specifically, sewing shut the eyes of a falcon or hawk at the moment of its capture, and the consequent loss or molting of its feathers during the taming process. The quadrilaterals قَرْنَس / قَرْنَص denote this concrete falconry operation, with the س and ص variants reflecting an ancient dialectal alternation on the فَعْلَل pattern.

-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: No established metaphorical, philosophical, or theological usage is attested in the classical lexica. The term remains confined to the concrete domain of falconry and avian husbandry; any extended application (e.g., to a person whose sight or insight is forcibly obstructed) would be an ad hoc rhetorical extension rather than a recognized majāz of the root.

ROOT قطا   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root ق ط و is the notion of heaviness and slowness in gait — a ponderous, dragging mode of walking. This sensory kernel of weighted movement is the root's primitive semantic nucleus, from which the name of the well-known bird (al-qaṭā) is derived by reason of its sluggish, heavy tread.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal, physical meaning is: to walk heavily and slowly (قَطَا يَقْطُو: ثَقُلَ مَشْيُهُ). The noun al-qaṭā denotes the well-known bird (sandgrouse), so named because of the heaviness of its walking; its singular is qaṭāh, and its plural forms are qaṭawāt and qaṭayāt. Its characteristic manner of walking is termed al-iqṭīṭāʾ, expressed in the verb iqṭawṭat al-qaṭāh (the sandgrouse walked in its heavy, dragging fashion).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: No securely attested metaphorical, philosophical, or theological usage is recorded in the classical lexical tradition for this root. The semantic field remains confined to the literal domain of heavy, slow locomotion and the ornithonym derived from it. ROOT قها   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is the withdrawal or recession of appetite from food — a turning-away of the desiring faculty from nourishment, whether from satiety, distaste, or a constitutional diminution of hunger, without organic disease.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning is the cessation or retreat of the appetite for food, such that one turns away from eating; it is said of a man of little food-intake: qad aqhā wa-qad aqham.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In scholastic and ethical usage, the root denotes the deliberate withholding of the appetitive faculty from what is present and desired — the case in which one is able to eat but refrains though still craving it. This is distinguished from the pathological loss of appetite and from the mere absence of hunger; it thus borders on the ethical discipline of restraining the concupiscible appetite (al-shahwah) and is compared to aqham, which carries the additional sense of aversion or loathing.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: أَقْهى عن الطعام واقْتَهى: ارتدَّت شهوتُه عنه من غير مرض، مثل أَقْهَمَ؛ يقال للرجل القليل الطُّعم: قد أَقْهَى وقد أَقْهَم، وقيل: هو أَن يقدر على الطعام فلا يأَكله وإِن كان مشتهياً له.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Aqhā ʿan al-ṭaʿām and iqtahā: his appetite receded from it without disease, like aqham; it is said of a man of little food-intake: qad aqhā wa-qad aqham. It is also said to be that he is able to eat but does not eat it, even though he craves it.
