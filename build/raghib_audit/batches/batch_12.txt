ROOT نكد   stratum=MA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin of the root ن ك د is the notion of something sought or desired being obtained only with difficulty, hardship, or scarcity — a thing that resists its seeker and yields its benefit grudgingly. From this core emerges the associated sense of ill-omen, meanness, and depletion.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: In its literal physical sense, ن ك د denotes a thing that comes to its seeker only with hardship and difficulty. It is applied to a man as نَكِد (niggardly, ill-tempered, of evil omen), to a she-camel as نَكْدَاء (one whose milk is scant and whose milking is difficult), and to water as نَكِدَ (exhausted, drained). It also denotes the act of exhausting someone's resources through persistent asking, and the depletion of a thing entirely.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and scholastically, ن ك د is used to describe: (1) anything that brings evil or harm to its possessor, hence ill-omen and meanness (الشؤم واللؤم); (2) a life or livelihood that becomes harsh and constricted (نَكِدَ عيشُهم); (3) a gift or bestowal that is meager and unenjoyable (عطاء منكود ومنكد); (4) the act of spoiling a gift by reminding the recipient of it (نكد عطاءه بالمن); (5) the raven's exhaustive croaking as if vomiting (نكد الغراب وتنكد); and (6) in the Qur'anic verse 'والذي خبث لا يخرج إلا نكدا' (Q 7:58), the base and impure yields only scant, unprofitable produce — a theological metaphor for the barrenness of disbelief and the meagerness of its fruits.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: النَّكَدُ: الشُّؤْمُ وَاللُّؤْمُ، وَكُلُّ شَيْءٍ خَرَجَ إِلَى طَالِبِهِ بِتَعَسُّرٍ، وَكُلُّ شَيْءٍ جَرَّ عَلَى صَاحِبِهِ شَرًّا فَهُوَ نَكَدٌ.

ROOT نول   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) centers on the act of giving, bestowing, or reaching out something to someone, particularly a benefit or favor, and by extension, the reception or attainment of that benefit. It is used in theological contexts to describe God's bestowal of grace and in philosophical discourse regarding the acquisition of knowledge or virtue. The term 'مناولة' in ḥadīth transmission denotes the direct handing over of a text, which is considered a high form of authorization (ijāza).

ROOT نوي   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is the act of directing oneself toward a place or object with deliberate intent, specifically the setting of one's course toward a destination or the resolve to undertake a journey. It denotes the orienting of the self toward a goal, whether spatial (a dwelling, a land) or volitional (an intention, a belief).
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: According to Asās al-Balāghah, the literal physical meaning is: a people's directing themselves toward a dwelling in a certain place, and their intending it (نوى القوم منزلا بمكان كذا وانتووه); also, the forming of a resolve to travel or to be absent from one's homeland (نوى غربة), and the statement 'I intend your intention' meaning 'I intend to travel with you and accompany you' (وأنا نويك أي نويت المسافرة معك ومرافقتك).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorical, philosophical, and theological usages: The root extends from spatial orientation to volitional and cognitive acts. It signifies the deliberate positing of an intention (niyyah) as the orienting principle of action, the mental positing of a belief or doctrine (iʿtiqād), and the resolve of the soul toward a desired end. In scholastic theology and philosophy, niyyah is the soul's directedness toward an act, constituting the moral and intentional core of human agency; it is the inward orientation that determines the value of outward acts. The term also denotes the faculty of intention itself, the object of intention, and the act of intending, bridging the physical act of journeying and the metaphysical act of willing.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: نَوَى الشيءَ نِيَّةً ونِيَةً: قصده واعتقده. ونوى المنزل وانْتَواه كذلك. والنِّيَّةُ: الوجه يُذْهَب فيه.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: He intended the thing with an intention (niyyah) and a resolve (niyah): he aimed at it and believed in it. He intended the dwelling and likewise directed himself toward it. And niyyah is the direction toward which one goes.

ROOT همي   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root همي is the free, downward, and continuous flow of a liquid—specifically tears, rain, or blood—from a source, and by extension, the swift, unresisted departure or movement of a being or object.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is the pouring forth and flowing of tears from the eye, rain from the sky, or blood from the mouth of a horse; also, the going away or departing of camels upon their faces.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorical and extended usages include: the flowing of any liquid substance; the swift departure or vanishing of a thing; and the nominal form هِمْيَان (himyān) denoting a waist-belt or purse, derived from the notion of something that flows or hangs loosely. The root is explicitly distinguished from هَامَ (to wander in love or madness), as the classical lexicographers note that هَمَى is not related to هَائِم.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: هَمَتْ عينُه هَمْياً وهُمِيًّا وهَمَياناً: صَبَّتْ دمعها، وقيل: سالَ دَمْعُها، وكذلك كلُّ سائل من مطر وغيره. وليس هذا من الهائم في شيء.

-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and theologically, the root is applied to God as Al-Wakīl (الوكيل), meaning the One who independently manages the affairs of His servants, guarantees their sustenance, and is sufficient for them. In this sense, it transcends the literal notion of delegation, as God is not appointed by anyone but is inherently the Disposer of all affairs. The verbal form تَوَكَّلَ (to rely upon) is used in two distinct ways: to rely upon God (توكل على الله) in absolute trust, and to rely upon others in a blameworthy manner (تواكل), which is discouraged. The term also appears in legal and commercial contexts, such as appointing an agent for a sale (وَكَّلْتُهُ بِالْبَيْعِ).

ROOT وهن   stratum=MA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is the diminution of structural integrity or vital force in a living body or solid object, specifically the loss of load-bearing capacity in bone, limb, or constitution, such that the entity can no longer sustain effort or resist pressure.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: Literal physical weakness or enervation in bodily structure, action, or resolve; the actual slackening of bone and sinew, as in 'the bone within me has weakened' (Q 19:4), and the palpable loss of strength in a person or animal that renders them incapable of rising or exerting force.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorical and theological applications include: (1) moral or spiritual enervation before the enemy or in the pursuit of justice, as in 'do not weaken and do not grieve' (Q 3:139) and 'they did not weaken in the face of what befell them' (Q 3:146); (2) the compounding of gestational burden, 'weakness upon weakness' (Q 31:14), denoting the successive depletion of the mother's strength through pregnancy and childbirth; (3) divine action upon the plots of disbelievers, 'God is the one who weakens the stratagems of the disbelievers' (Q 8:18), where the root signifies the ontological nullification of causal efficacy; (4) temporal usage, 'a while after nightfall' (وَهْنًا وَمُوهِنًا), by extension from the slackening of the day's activity; and (5) the dual form الوَاهِنَتَانِ for the two short ribs flanking the heart, as the locus of structural vulnerability in the thorax.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الوَهْن: الضَّعْف في العمل والأمر، وكذلك في العَظْم ونحوه؛ وقد وَهَنَ يَهِنُ وَوَهِنَ يَوْهَنُ، وأَوْهَنَهُ غيره ووَهَّنَهُ. وفي التنزيل: حَمَلَتْهُ أُمُّهُ وَهْنًا على وَهْنٍ، أي ضَعْفًا على ضَعْفٍ.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-wahn is weakness in action and in matters, as well as in bone and the like; the verb occurs as wahana yahinu and wahina yawhanu, with the transitive forms awhanahu and wahhanahu. In the revelation: 'His mother carried him in weakness upon weakness' (Q 31:14), that is, debility compounded upon debility.
