ROOT ضغغ   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) centers on a lush, verdant, well-watered meadow (rawḍah) that is fresh, tender, and free of blemish or decay—a self-contained, flourishing garden spot. The underlying sensory image is that of soft, moist, thriving herbage, from which the root's nominal form al-ḍaghīghah is derived.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning, per Lisān al-ʿArab, is a fresh, blooming, unblemished meadow or garden (al-rawḍah al-nāḍirah al-mutakhalliyah), also called al-ḍaghīghah, al-marghadah, al-maghmaghah, al-makhjalah, al-marghah, and al-ḥadīqah. Abū Ḥanīfah reports the idiom: 'They are in a ḍaghīghah of the ḍaghāḍigh'—i.e., in a state of lush, abundant, and pleasant verdure.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: No attested metaphorical, philosophical, or theological usage is recorded in the classical lexica for this root. The term remains confined to its concrete botanical and topographical sense. Any extended application—such as describing a person or community as being 'in a ḍaghīghah'—is idiomatic and rhetorical, signifying ease, prosperity, or luxuriant well-being, but it does not constitute a formal majāz in the scholastic or theological register.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الضَّغِيغَةُ: الرَّوْضَةُ النَّاضِرَةُ المُتَخَلِّيَةُ. وَيُقَالُ: هُمْ فِي ضَغِيغَةٍ مِنَ الضَّغَاضِغِ إِذَا كَانُوا فِي رِيَاضٍ مُعْشِبَةٍ نَاعِمَةٍ.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-ḍaghīghah: a fresh, blooming, unblemished meadow. It is said: 'They are in a ḍaghīghah of the ḍaghāḍigh' when they are amid lush, tender, herbaceous gardens.

ROOT ضفع   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root ض-ف-ع is the emission of excrement or the act of defecation, specifically denoting the expulsion of waste from the body. It is a primary, concrete bodily function, with a variant root فضع carrying the same meaning.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is the act of defecating or excreting, as in ضَفَعَ الرجلُ (the man defecated). It also extends to the emission of urine and dung, as in ضَفَعَ بِبَوْلِهِ وَسَلَحَ (he urinated and defecated).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: While the root is primarily literal and concrete, it may be used metaphorically in scholastic or theological contexts to denote the expulsion of waste or impurity, symbolizing moral or spiritual corruption. However, no attested majāz usage is recorded in the classical sources; the term remains within the domain of physical bodily functions.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: ضَفَعَ الرجلُ يَضْفَعُ ضَفْعاً: جَعَسَ وأَحْدَثَ، وقيل: أَبْدَى، وفَضَعَ لغة فيه. ويقال: ضَفَعَ وقَعَ بِبَوْلِه وسَلَحَ. وقال ابن الأَعرابي: نَجْوُ الفيل الضَّفْعُ.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: A man defecated, he excreted and produced waste; it is also said: he exposed [excrement]. فَضَعَ is a variant dialect form. It is said: he defecated, he urinated and defecated. Ibn al-Aʿrābī said: The dung of the elephant is called al-ḍafʿ.

The parallel cited by Abū Manṣūr, بَضَى as a transposition of بَاضَ, confirms that the phenomenon is phonological rearrangement rather than semantic figuration.

ROOT ضود   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root ضود is not attested as an independent lexical root in the classical philological tradition; the sequence ض و د appears in Lisān al-ʿArab only as an incidental collocation within the entry for the letter ḍād (ض), where the letter itself is treated as a phonological and orthographic primitive rather than as a semantic root. Its ontological core is therefore the consonantal letter ḍād as a majhūra (voiced) and mustaʿliya (elevated) Arabic phoneme, exclusive to Arabic speech.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: No literal physical meaning is established for ضود as a root in Asās al-Balāghah or in the classical lexical corpus; the only literal referent attested is the letter ḍād (ض) itself, described as a voiced, elevated Arabic consonant that functions as an aṣl (radical) and never as a badal (substitute) or zāʾid (augment).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In scholastic and philological usage, the discussion of ض is metaphorical or metalinguistic rather than semantic: the letter is said to be khāṣṣa li-l-ʿArab (peculiar to the Arabs), absent from non-Arab speech except rarely, and this exclusivity is cited in exegetical and rhetorical commentary, including on the verse of Abū al-Ṭayyib. No theological or philosophical majāz is attested for the sequence ضود as a root.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الضاد حرف هجاء وهو حرف مَجْهُور، وهو أَحد الحروف المُسْتَعْلية، يكون أَصلاً لا بدلاً ولا زائداً، والضاد للعَرَب خاصة ولا توجد في كلام العجم إِلا في القليل.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: The ḍād is a letter of spelling; it is a voiced consonant and one of the elevated letters; it functions as a radical, not as a substitute or an augment; and the ḍād is peculiar to the Arabs, not occurring in non-Arab speech except rarely.

ROOT طلس   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin of the root ط ل س is the act of effacing, obliterating, or wiping out written traces from a surface, and the resulting state of a document or hide whose markings have been erased or obscured. From this primary sense of erasure and obscuration, the root extends to denote a dusty, ash-grey, or dusky coloration that obscures the natural brightness of a thing, as in the grey wolf (ذئب أطلس).
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is the effacement of writing: طَلَسَ الكتابَ means to erase the book, and طَلَّسَهُ means to efface it thoroughly so as to corrupt its script. When the effacement is complete and the writing is reduced to a blank, superfluous surface, it becomes a طِرْس. The physical instrument of erasure is the طَلَاسَة, a rag or cloth used to wipe the tablet. Literally, it also denotes a dusty, greyish coloration, as in ذئب أطلس, a grey wolf, and ذئاب طلس, grey wolves.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In metaphorical and scholastic usage, the root denotes the obliteration of intellectual or spiritual traces, the obscuring of clear evidence, and the effacement of identity or reputation. The phrase خرج القاضي متقلسا متطلسا describes a judge who goes out wearing a طيلسان (a type of cloak) and covering himself, deriving from the sense of concealment and overlaying. The term طَلْس is used as a variant of طِرْس for a erased or blank sheet, and by extension, for any surface or mind devoid of legible content. The greyness of the wolf (أطلس) metaphorically signifies a dusky, indistinct appearance that conceals the animal's form, paralleling the obscuration of writing.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الطَّلْسُ: لغة في الطِّرْس، وهو المَحْوُ. طَلَسَ الكتابَ طَلْساً وطَلَّسَهُ فَتَطَلَّسَ: كَطَرَّسَهُ. ويقال للصحيفة إذا محيت: طِلْسٌ وطِرْسٌ. والذئب الأطلس: الأغبر.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-ṭals is a variant of al-ṭirs, and it means effacement. He erased the book with a single act of erasure (ṭalasa) or with intensive erasure (ṭallasa), so it became erased (taṭallasa), just as one says ṭarrasa. A sheet, when it is effaced, is called ṭils and ṭirs. The wolf that is aṭlas is the dusty, ash-grey one.

ROOT طوغ   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root ط-و-غ denotes transgression, overflow, and exceeding the proper bound — the foundational physical image being that of a torrent or flood that bursts its channel and overruns its limits, hence any entity that oversteps the measure of the created order.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: In its strict literal (ḥaqīqah) sense, طاغوت designates any object of worship set up alongside or instead of God — idols, the Devil, soothsayers, or human authorities who claim absolute obedience — insofar as each has transgressed (ṭaghā) the bound of creatureliness and usurped a prerogative belonging to the Divine alone.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Scholastically and theologically, طاغوت is extended (majāz) to every 'head' or leader in error (raʾs fī al-ḍalāl), every false claimant to authority, and every system of thought or governance that commands allegiance contrary to divine law. The Qurʾānic pairing with al-jibt (يؤمنون بالجبت والطاغوت) marks it as the archetype of false transcendence — the metaphysical counterfeit of the Divine — and in later kalām and uṣūl al-fiqh it functions as a technical term for any authority whose command is obeyed in defiance of the Sharīʿah.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الطَّاغُوتُ: ما عُبِدَ من دون الله عز وجل، وكلُّ رَأْسٍ في الضلالِ طاغوتٌ؛ وقيل: الأَصْنامُ، وقيل: الشيطانُ، وقيل: الكَهَنةُ، وقيل: مَرَدةُ أَهل الكتاب.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-ṭāghūt: that which is worshipped besides God — Mighty and Majestic — and every leader in error is a ṭāghūt; it is said to be idols, or the Devil, or soothsayers, or the rebellious ones among the People of the Book.

ROOT طول   stratum=Ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is spatial extension in a single dimension — the quality of a body, object, or duration being drawn out in length, standing in direct opposition (نقيض) to shortness (قِصَر). This primary corporeal sense of linear elongation is the root from which all temporal, quantitative, and moral extensions are derived.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning is the contrary of shortness (نقيض القِصَر) in people and other beings, whether animate or inanimate: the state of a thing being extended in length. It applies to concrete entities (aʿyān) such as a body, a rope, or a horse's tether, and to the act of lengthening or letting out (e.g., طَوِّلْ فَرَسَكَ, 'let out your horse's tether').
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: By extension from spatial length, the root is applied metaphorically to temporal duration (الزمان), as in فَطَالَ عَلَيْهِمُ الأَمَدُ ('the term was prolonged for them') and سَبْحًا طَوِيلًا ('a long course of swimming'). It is further transferred to abstract and moral domains: الطَّوْل denotes superabundance, favor, and beneficence (الفضل والمن), as in ذِي الطَّوْلِ ('possessor of bounty'); it signifies wealth and capacity (سعة المال والقدرة), as in أُولُو الطُّولِ ('those of ample means') and مَنْ لَمْ يَسْتَطِعْ مِنْكُمْ طَوْلًا ('those who cannot afford'), a metonymy for what is spent on dower and maintenance. تَطَاوَلَ means to manifest length or to claim superiority, as in فَتَطَاوَلَ عَلَيْهِمُ الْعُمُرُ ('the age was prolonged over them'). طَوَالَ الدَّهْرِ denotes the duration of time.

ROOT علا   stratum=Ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin of the root is vertical elevation and height above a baseline, conceived as the spatial opposite of lowness (sifl). It denotes the act of rising, ascending, or being positioned above something, whether in physical space or in rank.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal, physical meaning is height, elevation, or the upper part of a thing. It is used primarily for spatial and corporeal elevation, as in 'he rose above the place' or 'the upper part of the house' (ʿilw al-dār). The verb ʿalā (يَعْلُو) is used for both praiseworthy and blameworthy elevation, whereas ʿaliya (يَعْلَى) is reserved for praiseworthy elevation only.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and theologically, the root extends to elevation in rank, status, or power. It can denote arrogance and tyrannical self-exaltation, as in Pharaoh's 'exalting himself in the land' (Q 28:4) and the 'haughty' people (Q 23:46). It also signifies the transcendent elevation of God, and in scholastic contexts, the ontological superiority of causes over effects. The term ʿulw (height) is contrasted with sufl (lowness), and ʿulwī/ suflī designate the celestial and terrestrial realms respectively.
