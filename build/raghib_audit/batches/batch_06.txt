ROOT شبث   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root ش-ب-ث is the act of clinging, adhering, or becoming physically attached to an object, such that it is seized and held fast. The root denotes a concrete, tactile grasping or entanglement—a thing becoming caught upon or fastened to another.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: According to Asās al-Balāghah, the literal physical meaning is: to cling to something, to hold fast to it, and to become attached to it. The phrase تشبث به (tashabbatha bihi) denotes direct, physical clinging or adherence. The related form شابثه (shābathahu) indicates mutual clinging or grappling. The noun شبث (shabath) and its plural شبثان (shubthān) refer to a thing that clings or is clung to, with the image of فرنده مدارج شبثان (faranduhu madārij shubthān)—its edge or fringe being like the winding paths of clinging things—evoking a concrete, physical entanglement.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In metaphorical and scholastic usage, the root extends from physical clinging to intellectual and psychological attachment. Ibn al-Aʿrābī's statement ما أَدْري مِن أَين شَبِثْتُها (I do not know from where I seized it) employs the root to denote the grasping of a poetic verse or idea—an intellectual apprehension and retention. Similarly, التَّشَبُّثُ بالشيء (al-tashabbuthu bi-l-shayʾ) is used for emotional or conceptual attachment to a thing, and التَّثَبُّتُ (al-tathabbuth) is glossed as التَعَلُّق (al-taʿalluq), i.e., attachment or dependence. The root thus moves from the physical clinging of bodies to the mental clinging of concepts, memories, and affections, and further to the theological notion of the soul's attachment to worldly things or to the divine.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: شَبِثَ الشيءَ: عَلِقَه وأَخذه. والتَّشَبُّثُ بالشيء: التَّعَلُّق به. والتَّثَبُّتُ: التَّعَلُّق.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: He clung to the thing: he became attached to it and took hold of it. Clinging to a thing is attachment to it. And attachment is clinging.

ROOT شثل   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) denotes coarse, thick, and densely compacted bodily tissue — specifically the flesh of the fingers, hands, and feet — characterized by roughness of texture and a knotted, interlocking mass. The primary sensory image is that of a limb whose flesh is not smooth or supple but hard, rough, and closely packed.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning, as attested in Lisān al-ʿArab, is a man whose fingers are thick and rough (شَثْل الأَصابع: غليظُها خَشِنُها), and a foot that is thick-fleshed and densely compacted (قَدَمٌ شَثْلةٌ: غليظةُ اللحم مُتَراكِبةٌ). The verb شَثِلَتْ يَدُهُ ورِجْلُهُ describes the hand or foot becoming thus thick, rough, and densely fleshed.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: No metaphorical, philosophical, or theological extension is attested in the classical lexicographical tradition. The root remains confined to the concrete, sensory domain of bodily texture. Ibn Manẓūr records the view of Yaʿqūb and Abū ʿUbayd that the final radical lām is a substitute (badal) for the nūn of شَثْن, making شَثْل a dialectal variant (lughah) of شَثْن, as explicitly stated by Ibn al-Sikkīt. This substitution is phonological, not semantic; it does not generate a distinct majāz. The root therefore possesses no scholastic or abstract application in the classical sources.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: رجل شَثْل الأَصابع: غليظُها خَشِنُها. وقَدَمٌ شَثْلةٌ: غليظةُ اللحم مُتَراكِبةٌ. والشَّثْل لغة في الشَّثْن.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: A man with thick and rough fingers. And a foot that is thick-fleshed and densely compacted. And shathl is a dialectal variant of shathn.

ROOT شري   stratum=Ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is the reciprocal transaction of exchange: the transfer of a commodity against a price, such that one party surrenders the object and receives the price while the other surrenders the price and receives the object. The root's primary semantic nucleus is the act of exchanging one entity for another, whether by sale or purchase.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning is the act of buying and selling, i.e., the exchange of a commodity for a price. In this literal sense, the buyer (al-mushtarī) pays the price and takes the commodity, while the seller (al-bāʾiʿ) delivers the commodity and takes the price. When the exchange is barter (commodity for commodity), each party can simultaneously be characterized as buyer and seller. The verb sharanī can mean either 'he bought from me' or 'he sold to me' depending on context, though shara (شَرَى) is more commonly used for 'he sold' and ibtāʿa (اِبْتَاعَ) for 'he bought'.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorical and theological usages extend the exchange paradigm to non-commercial domains: (1) In the Qurʾān, 'purchasing' (ishtarā) is used for the exchange of one's life, faith, or covenant for divine pleasure or worldly gain, e.g., 'Who sells his life seeking the pleasure of God' (Q 2:207), 'They sell the life of this world for the Hereafter' (Q 4:74), 'Those who purchase a small gain at the cost of God's covenant' (Q 3:77). (2) The root is applied to any acquisition or exchange of intangible goods, such as guidance for error, or punishment for forgiveness. (3) In scholastic theology, the metaphor conveys the deliberate transaction of one's ultimate allegiance, whether to God or to worldly interests, emphasizing volitional exchange and moral accountability.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: شَرَى الشيءَ يَشْرِيه شِرًى وشِرَاءً واشْتَرَاهُ سَوَاءٌ، وشَرَاهُ واشْتَرَاهُ: بَاعَهُ. والشراء والبيع يتلازمان، فالمشتري دافع الثمن وآخذ المثمن، والبائع دافع المثمن وآخذ الثمن.

ROOT شطن   stratum=MA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is spatial and relational estrangement: to be far removed, distant, or remote. This is exemplified by the long, tightly twisted rope (شَطَن) used to draw water from a deep well or to tether a horse, and by the deep well (بئر شطون) whose bottom is far from its mouth. A secondary, disputed origin is combustion (شاط يشيط): to burn with rage, from which the Satanic being is said to be created from fire.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is the long, strong, tightly twisted rope used for drawing water or tethering an animal; a deep well (بئر شطون); a distant house (شطنت الدار); and a far-reaching journey or estrangement (غربة شطون). It also denotes the physical act of a horse resisting and being bound with two ropes from opposite sides (لينزو بين شطنين).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorical, philosophical, and theological usages: (1) The being known as al-Shayṭān, whether from the root meaning 'to be distant' (from divine mercy) or from 'to burn' (with rage), created from fire, characterized by extreme irascible power and blameworthy zeal, who refused to prostrate to Adam. (2) Any rebellious, insolent being among jinn, humans, or animals (as per Abū ʿUbayda). (3) The human associates or leaders of evil (شياطين الإنس والجن). (4) The act of behaving in a devilish, rebellious, or insolent manner (تشيطن، فيه شيطنة). (5) The intense, uncontrollable restlessness of a horse compared to a devil (كأنه شيطان).
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الشَّطَنُ: الحَبْلُ الطَّوِيلُ الشَّدِيدُ الفَتْلِ يُسْتَقى به وتُشَدُّ به الخَيْلُ، والجمع أَشْطان. وشَطَنَتِ الدارُ: بَعُدَتْ. والشَّيْطانُ: اسمٌ لكلِّ عارِمٍ من الجِنِّ والإِنْسِ والحيواناتِ، وقيل: هو من شَطَنَ أَي تَباعَدَ، وقيل: من شاطَ يَشيطُ احْتَرَقَ غَضَباً.

Al-Shayṭān: a name for every rebellious, insolent being among jinn, humans, and animals. ROOT شكا   stratum=Ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is the opening of a small water-skin (shakwah) to expose its contents; this concrete image of revealing what is contained within a vessel underlies all subsequent usage.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: To disclose or expose one's inner distress or grievance; literally, to open a small water-skin and reveal what it holds.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorical and theological applications: (1) To complain or lament before God, as in 'I only complain of my anguish and grief to Allah' (Q 12:86) and 'She complains to Allah' (Q 58:1); (2) To cause someone to complain, i.e., to give them reason for complaint (ashkāhu); (3) To remove someone's complaint or grievance (ashkāhu); (4) The niche (mishkāt) as a non-piercing lamp recess, symbolizing the heart that contains the light of God (Q 24:35).

-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Complaint, grievance, and lamentation: the disclosure of inner distress. The root origin of complaint is the opening of a small water-skin and the exposure of its contents; it is a small vessel used for water. The mishkāt is a non-piercing niche.

ROOT شها   stratum=Ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is the soul's innate inclination and appetitive pull toward what it desires, rooted in the body's need for what sustains it.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning is the soul's appetitive inclination toward an object of desire, specifically the bodily need for what is indispensable to health, such as the craving for food when hungry.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and scholastically, it denotes the appetitive faculty itself; it is divided into true desire (what the body requires) and false desire (what is superfluous). It is applied to the eschatological reward of the Garden, where believers obtain whatever their souls desire, and to blameworthy worldly cravings that lead to transgression.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: أصل الشهوة: نزوع النفس إلى ما تريده، وذلك في الدنيا ضربان: صادقة، وكاذبة، فالصادقة: ما يختل البدن من دونه كشهوة الطعام عند الجوع، والكاذبة: ما لا يختل من دونه.

ROOT شور   stratum=MA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root شور is the act of extracting, exposing, or bringing something hidden into plain view — most concretely, the extraction of honey from its hive (شَارَ العَسَلَ) and the exposure of goods or a beast for inspection and sale. -- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning, per Asās al-Balāghah, is the extraction of honey from the hive (شَارَ العَسَلَ وَاشْتَارَهُ), and the leading forth of a riding beast or horse to be displayed and inspected for sale (شَرْتُ الدَّابَّةَ وَشَوَّرْتُهَا: عَرَضْتُهَا لِلْبَيْعِ). It also denotes the place or act of such display, the مِشْوَار (show-ring, parade ground), and the visible goods or wares (الشَّوَار: مَا يَبْدُو مِنَ الْمَتَاع).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and scholastically, the root extends to: (1) Consultation and counsel (شَاوَرَهُ، اسْتَشَارَهُ، تَشَاوَرُوا، اشتوروا) — the eliciting of a hidden opinion, as if extracting honey from its receptacle; the Quranic principle of شُورَى (mutual consultation) and the phrase وَأَمْرُهُمْ شُورَىٰ بَيْنَهُمْ derive from this. (2) The exposure of one's private parts (شَوَار, عَوْرَة) — as in the curse 'أَبْدَى اللهُ شَوَارَكَ' (May God expose your shame), and the proverbial 'أَشْوَارُ عَرُوسٍ تُرَى' (the bride's adornments are displayed to be seen). (3) The testing or probing of a horse's gait and character (شَوَّرَهَا يَنْظُرُ كَيْفَ مِشْوَارُهَا) — hence 'فَرَسٌ حَسَنُ الْمِشْوَارِ' (a horse of fine show and gait). (4) The indication or gesture (الإِشَارَة) — a pointing that reveals meaning without speech, and by extension 'رَجُلٌ حَسَنُ الشَّارَةِ' (a man of fine appearance and bearing). (5) In the Ḥadīth of al-Zabāʾ, the display of a bride's ornaments. The theological usage in 'أَبْدَى اللهُ شَوَارَكَ' is a curse invoking divine exposure of hidden shame, while the political-theological term شُورَى denotes the collective deliberation of the community in matters of governance, as when ʿUmar (رضي الله عنه) left the caliphate as a matter of شُورَى.

ROOT صهر   stratum=MA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is the melting and liquefying of fat (إذابة الشحم), from which flows the derived sense of a molten substance (صهارة) and, by extension, the dissolution of solidity through intense heat. From this concrete physical process, the root branches into the abstract domain of marital kinship, conceived as a bond that dissolves the boundaries between separate households and creates a new fused social entity.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is the act of melting fat (صهر الشحم: أذابه), its liquefied product (صهارة: ذوبه), and the use of that molten fat as a condiment for bread (صهر الخبز: أدمه بها) or as an ointment for the head (صهر رأسه: دهنه بالصهارة). It also denotes the concrete kinship relation itself: a man's in-law (صهر) and the family into which one marries (أصهار).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and scholastically, the root denotes the bond of marital kinship (حرمة الزواج) that unites families, as in the Qur'anic verse 'فجعله نسبا وصهرا' (Q 25:54), where 'صهر' signifies the affinal tie established by marriage. It extends to the state of being connected by marriage, lineage, or even neighborly protection (الإصهار: التحرم بجوار أو نسب أو تزوج). Theologically, the root is applied to the punishment of the damned in Q 22:20, 'يصهر به ما في بطونهم' — 'that which is in their bellies will be melted' — where the physical melting of fat becomes a metaphor for the dissolution of the body under divine chastisement. ROOT صيح   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin of the root ص-ي-ح is the emission of a loud, piercing sound, specifically a vocal cry that strains the utterer's maximum capacity. It denotes an intense auditory event, whether from a human, animal, or inanimate object, and by extension, the act of calling out to someone or something.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal, physical meaning is to shout, cry out, or emit a loud sound with one's utmost effort. It also encompasses calling out to someone (صاح به) and the reciprocal act of shouting at one another (تصايحوا). Additionally, it is used literally for the splitting or cracking of objects like a garment or a stick (انصاح الثوب, انصاحت العصا).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically, the root is applied to non-vocal entities, such as a palm tree or dates (صيحاني, صيحانية), which are named after a specific ram called صيحان, thus transferring the proper name to a botanical variety. The meaning of 'splitting' is extended to abstract disintegration or rupture. In theological and philosophical discourse, the term may be used to describe the 'crying out' of inanimate objects as a metaphor for their testimony or the manifestation of divine power, though such usage is rare and strictly metaphorical.

ROOT صيص   stratum=Ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) centers on the notion of a hard, protective, or defensive structure — a fortified enclosure, a horn, a spur, or a hardened date-pit — that serves as a means of defense or resistance.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah (as delineated via Lisān al-ʿArab) is: a fortress or stronghold; anything by which one fortifies oneself; specifically, the horn of a bull or the spur of a rooster; and in the dialect of Balḥārith ibn Kaʿb, the hardened, dry date-pit (ḥashaf).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorical, philosophical, and theological usages: In the Qurʾānic verse 'and He brought down those of the People of the Book who supported them from their strongholds' (Q 33:26), 'ṣayāṣī' (pl. of ṣīṣah) denotes their fortresses and places of refuge. The term is extended to any means of self-protection or defense, including the horn of a bull and the spur of a rooster, as these are instruments of defense or combat. The root also appears in the context of date palms: 'aṣāṣat al-nakhlah' and 'ṣayyaṣat taṣyīṣan' mean the palm became 'shīṣ' (unripe or hardened dates), derived from ṣīṣ (hardened date-pit), not from ṣīṣāʾ (a different root).
