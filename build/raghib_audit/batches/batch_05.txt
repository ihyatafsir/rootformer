ROOT زخرف   stratum=Ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is gold (al-dhahab), specifically gold as an object of ornamentation and embellishment; from this material substrate the root extends to denote any adornment, and thence to anything artificially beautified or falsified.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: Gold; then any ornament or embellishment (zīnah); then any embellished, gilded, or artificially beautified thing. The verb زَخْرَفَ denotes the act of adorning, embellishing, and perfecting a thing, as in زَخْرَفَ البيتَ (he adorned and completed the house).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorical and scholastic applications include: (1) embellished speech or discourse (زخرف القول) understood as deceptive, ornamented words that veil falsehood; (2) the gilded or golden chambers of worldly luxury (بيت من زخرف) signifying transient material splendor; (3) the earth's adornment (أخذت الأرض زخرفها) as the flourishing of worldly life that precedes its decay; (4) any falsified or counterfeit presentation (كل مموه مزور) in which surface beauty conceals corrupt reality. In theological and ethical usage, the term denotes the seductive ornamentation of worldly existence and the rhetorical embellishment of falsehood.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الزُّخْرُفُ: الزِّينَةُ، وأَصْلُهُ الذَّهَبُ، ثُمَّ سُمِّيَ كُلُّ زِينَةٍ زُخْرُفاً، ثُمَّ شُبِّهَ كُلُّ مُمَوَّهٍ مُزَوَّرٍ بِهِ. وَزَخْرَفَ البَيْتَ: زَيَّنَهُ وَأَكْمَلَهُ.

ROOT ززم   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root denotes a liminal, brackish state of water — neither purely fresh nor purely saline — situated at the threshold between two opposing qualities. Its reduplicative phonetic structure (ز-ز-م) mirrors this in-between condition, suggesting a substance that partakes of both extremes without fully belonging to either.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: Water that is neither fully salt nor fully sweet; brackish water occupying the intermediate zone between the saline and the potable.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: By extension, any entity, quality, or state that is suspended between two contraries, resisting categorical assignment to either pole. In scholastic and theological discourse, it may describe an ambiguous or indeterminate matter that partakes of two opposing natures without being reducible to either — a medial ontological status.

ROOT زلقم   stratum=ma
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) centers on the act of swallowing a morsel (لُقْمَة) — specifically, the passage of food through the throat — and by concrete extension, the anatomical throat itself (الحلقوم) and the snout or muzzle of a predatory animal (خُرْطوم الكلب والسبع), the orifice through which such swallowing occurs.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: The literal physical meaning according to the classical lexicographers is twofold: (1) the throat or gullet (الحلقوم) in certain Arabic dialects, and (2) the snout or muzzle of a dog or beast of prey (خُرْطوم الكلب والسبع). The verbal form زَلْقَمَ denotes the concrete act of swallowing a morsel (بَلَعَ اللُّقْمَةَ).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: No established metaphorical, philosophical, or theological usage is attested in the classical sources. The term remains confined to its concrete anatomical and physiological signification. Any extended application — such as employing الزُّلْقُوم for the throat as the seat of voice or for the beast's snout as an instrument of seizing — remains within the domain of literal anatomical reference rather than true majāz. The scholastic tradition records no abstract or figurative deployment of this root.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: الزُّلْقُومُ: الحلقوم في بعض اللغات، وخُرْطومُ الكلب والسبع. وزَلْقَمَ اللُّقْمَةَ: بَلَعَها.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-Zulqūm: the throat or gullet in certain dialects, and the snout of a dog or beast of prey. And zalqama al-luqma: he swallowed the morsel.

It denotes a sudden, unintended departure from a stable position or intended trajectory.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: According to Asās al-Balāghah, the literal meaning is: slipping from a rock or in mud (زل عن الصخرة وفي الطين زليلا); a place of slipping (مزلة); a man with a slipping foot or speech (سمع أزل); a woman who slips (امرأة زلاء); and God causing the earth to shake (زلزل الله الأرض زلزالا).
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and scholastically, the root extends to: (1) moral or doctrinal lapse—a slip in speech, judgment, or conduct (زل في منطقه); (2) theological usage in زلزال (earthquake) as a divine act of shaking or destabilizing; (3) philosophical application to any deviation from a norm, whether in logic, ethics, or natural motion; (4) the passive أزله (to cause someone to slip) implying external causation of error or downfall.

ROOT سبت   stratum=MA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin of the root is 'cutting' or 'severing' — the act of cutting off, whether hair, a journey, or activity. From this primary sense of severance, the root extends to the notion of rest (cessation of labor) and to tanned leather (from which hair has been removed).
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: Literal physical meanings: to cut off, to sever; to shave the head; to cut short a journey; to tan leather by removing its hair; tanned leather itself (especially that tanned with qaraẓ); the seventh day of the week (Saturday), named because God ceased creating on that day; and the state of being in Saturday or resting from work.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorical and theological applications: (1) Sleep as a 'cutting off' of activity, hence 'sabāt' (deep sleep or death) — 'We made your sleep a sabāt' (Q 78:9), meaning a severance from work, akin to the rest of night. (2) The Sabbath as a divinely ordained cessation of labor for the Jews, and their punishment for violating it (Q 7:163, 16:124). (3) 'Mabsūt' (passive participle) meaning dead, as in 'he became mabsūt' (i.e., his activities were cut off). (4) The verbal forms 'sabata' and 'asbata' meaning to observe the Sabbath or to enter upon it.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: السِّبْتُ: كُلُّ جِلْدٍ مَدْبُوغٍ، وَقِيلَ: هُوَ المَدْبُوغُ بِالقَرَظِ خَاصَّةً. وَأَصْلُ السَّبْتِ: القَطْعُ. وَسُبَات: النَّوْم، وَهُوَ قَطْعٌ لِلْعَمَلِ.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-Sibt: any tanned leather; it is said: specifically that tanned with qaraẓ. The root origin of 'sabt' is cutting. 'Subāt' is sleep, which is a cutting off of activity.

ROOT سحت   stratum=MA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is the act of peeling, scraping, or eradicating something down to its core—specifically, stripping away the outer layer (qishr) or fat from flesh, or shaving hair to the root. This concrete sense of total removal and uprooting is the root's primary material meaning.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: According to Asās al-Balāghah, the literal physical meaning is: to shave hair completely during a haircut or trimming, to peel fat away from flesh, to scrape the surface of the earth, and in circumcision, to perform the procedure thoroughly and exhaustively until the flesh is worn down.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: Metaphorically and scholastically, the root denotes anything forbidden, vile, or illicit that strips away a person's religion and moral integrity (murūʾah), such as bribery, unlawful earnings, the price of a dog, wine, or swine. It is used in the Qurʾān to describe those who consume illicit gain (akālūn li-l-suḥt) and in prophetic tradition: 'Every flesh nourished by illicit gain, the Fire is more deserving of it.' The term also extends to earnings that, while not strictly forbidden, compromise one's dignity, such as the cupper's fee, which the Prophet permitted to be used for feeding animals and slaves.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: السُّحْتُ والسُّحُتُ: كلُّ حرام قبيح الذِّكر؛ وقيل: هو ما خَبُثَ من المَكاسب وحَرُم فلَزِمَ عنه العارُ، وقَبيحُ الذِّكْر، كَثَمن الكلب والخمر والخنزير.

ROOT سحم   stratum=mA
-- RECORD.ontological_core: The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root سحم is the quality of deep, uniform blackness, specifically the saturated darkness exemplified by the raven's plumage and heavy rain-clouds; it denotes a blackness that is intrinsic and total rather than accidental or partial.
-- RECORD.haqiqah_literal: According to Asās al-Balāghah, the literal physical meaning is blackness (al-sawād), as in the phrase 'a raven that is asḥam' (ghurāb asḥam), 'black clouds' (saḥāb asḥam), and 'a black cloud-mass' (ghamāma saḥmāʾ); the verbal form saḥamū is used literally for darkening or blackening a face.
-- RECORD.majaz_scholastic: In scholastic and theological usage, the root is applied metaphorically to denote moral or spiritual blackening, as in the ḥadīth of the malediction (liʿān) where a child born 'asḥam aḥtam' signifies a deep, undeniable blackness that may imply illegitimacy or moral stain; likewise, in the ḥadīth of Abū Dharr, the term describes a person of dark complexion, extending the physical quality to human appearance without metaphorical abstraction. The root is not attested in Al-Mufradāt, so its ontological origin is derived from classical lexical sources.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.ar: السَّحَمُ والسُّحَامُ والسُّحْمَةُ: السواد، وكل أسود أسحم.
-- RECORD.canonical_definition.en: Al-saḥam, al-suḥām, and al-suḥma denote blackness; everything black is asḥam.
