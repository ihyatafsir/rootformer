REC ontological_core : The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) centers on fixedness and settled persistence in a place, with a secondary concrete sense of a boil or pustule reaching full maturation and becoming engorged with pus.

ROOT ملز   stratum=ma
REC ontological_core : The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root م-ل-ز is the act of slipping away, escaping, or quietly departing from a place, grasp, or state. It denotes a subtle, often elusive motion of withdrawal or release, frequently paired with its phonetic sibling م-ل-س (malasa), which carries the same semantic field of slipping out or slipping away.
REC haqiqah_literal  : The literal physical meaning is: a thing slipped away from me, departed, or went off (مَلَزَ الشيءُ عَنِّي); and one escaped or got free from a matter (امَّلَزَ من الأمر), or extracted oneself from it (تَمَلَّزَ من الأمر). The transitive form مَلَّزْتُه means: I caused it to slip away or made it depart.
REC canon_def.ar     : مَلَزَ الشيءُ عَنِّي مَلْزاً وامَّلَزَ ومَلَّزَ: ذهب. وتَمَلَّزَ من الأَمر تَمَلُّزاً وتَمَلَّسَ تَمَلُّساً: خرج منه. وامَّلَزَ من الأَمر وامَّلَسَ إِذا انفلت. وقد مَلَّزْتُه ومَلَّسْتُه إِذا فعلت به ذلك.

ROOT ناث   stratum=ma
REC ontological_core : The root ن-و-ث (n-w-th) denotes the fundamental act of agitating, shaking, or loosening a thing from its compacted or settled state, and by extension, the scattering or dispersing of its constituent parts.
REC haqiqah_literal  : To shake or agitate something so as to loosen it, or to scatter and disperse it; said of a thing that is stirred up and broken apart.
REC canon_def.ar     : نَاثَ الشَّيْءَ يَنِيثُهُ نَيْثًا: حَرَّكَهُ وَفَرَّقَهُ. وَالنَّيْثُ: التَّحْرِيكُ وَالتَّفْرِيقُ.
REC canon_def.en     : He shook the thing and dispersed it; al-nayth is the act of shaking and dispersing.

ROOT نهتر   stratum=ma
REC ontological_core : The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is not attested in the classical lexicons as a primary sensory or material root; it is a quadriliteral (rubāʿī) verbal root whose earliest lexical attestation is the act of speaking falsehood, specifically fabricating and circulating lies against others.
REC haqiqah_literal  : The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is not attested; based on Lisān al-ʿArab, the literal meaning is the act of speaking falsehood and lying, as in نَهْتَرَ عَلَيْنا (he fabricated lies against us).
REC canon_def.ar     : النَّهْتَرَةُ: التَّحَدُّثُ بِالكَذِبِ، وَقَدْ نَهْتَرَ عَلَيْنا.
REC canon_def.en     : Al-nahtarah: speaking falsehood; and he fabricated lies against us.

ROOT هنبث   stratum=ma
REC ontological_core : The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is not attested in Al-Mufradāt; classical lexicography derives it from the semantic field of tangled, interwoven, and confused affairs, with the concrete image of a knot or a dense thicket underlying the abstract notion of calamities and mixed reports.
REC haqiqah_literal  : The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is not attested; based on Lisān al-ʿArab, the ḥaqīqah is a single calamity or a tangled, confused matter (هَنْبَثَةٌ), and its plural (هَنَابِثُ) denotes calamities, affairs, and reports that are intermingled and obscure.
REC canon_def.ar     : الهَنَابِثُ: الدَّوَاهِي، واحدتها هَنْبَثَةٌ؛ وقيل: الهَنَابِثُ الأُمور والأَخْبار المختلطة.
REC canon_def.en     : Al-hanābith: calamities, the singular of which is hanbathah; it is also said that al-hanābith are confused affairs and reports.

ROOT وعم   stratum=ma
REC ontological_core : The root وعم denotes the act of addressing a dwelling-place or ruin with the imperative of well-being and enduring prosperity, rooted in the physical practice of saluting abandoned campsites and invoking blessing upon them.
REC haqiqah_literal  : To say to a house or ruin 'may you thrive and prosper' (انْعِمي), addressing it directly with the imperative of well-being.
REC canon_def.ar     : يقال: وَعَمْتُ الدارَ أَعِمُ وَعْمًا، أي قلت لها انْعِمي.
REC canon_def.en     : It is said: I addressed the dwelling with the salutation 'may you thrive' — that is, I said to it 'thrive and prosper.'
