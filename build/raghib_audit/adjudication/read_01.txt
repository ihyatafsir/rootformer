REC ontological_core : The foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) of the root قمح is the emergence and maturation of wheat-grain within the ear of corn, specifically from the onset of ripening until the grain is fully formed and compacted. From this concrete agricultural image, the root extends to the physical act of raising the head, particularly in refusal or aversion, as when a camel lifts its head away from water.

ROOT قنع   stratum=MA
REC ontological_core : The root's foundational physical origin (Aṣl al-Wadʿ) is the act of inclining or bending toward something, specifically the lowering or turning of the head or hand toward an object, as when a camel lowers its head to drink or a person extends a hand in supplication. This concrete image of orientation toward a source underlies all subsequent semantic developments.
REC haqiqah_literal  : The literal physical meaning according to Asās al-Balāghah is the act of bending or inclining the head, hand, or a vessel toward something, such as a camel lowering its head to a watering trough, a man extending his hands in prayer, or tilting a container to receive water from a stream. It also denotes the physical posture of a mouth with teeth inclined inward.

ROOT نسل   stratum=MA
REC ontological_core : The foundational physical origin of the root is the act of something separating, detaching, or falling away from a larger body or surface—such as hair, fur, or feathers shedding from an animal, or a garment slipping off a person. This core image of detachment and outward emission underlies all subsequent derivations.
REC haqiqah_literal  : The literal, physical meaning is the falling or shedding of hair, fur, or feathers from an animal, or the slipping off of a garment from a person. It denotes a concrete, observable separation of a part from its whole.

ROOT طول   stratum=Ma

This primary corporeal sense of linear elongation is the root from which all temporal, quantitative, and moral extensions are derived.
REC haqiqah_literal  : The literal physical meaning is the contrary of shortness (نقيض القِصَر) in people and other beings, whether animate or inanimate: the state of a thing being extended in length. It applies to concrete entities (aʿyān) such as a body, a rope, or a horse's tether, and to the act of lengthening or letting out (e.g., طَوِّلْ فَرَسَكَ, 'let out your horse's tether').
